--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION GENERATION & MULTI-SOURCE INGRESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Motivation Engine generates motivational states from twelve distinct internal and external origin pathways, operating either in isolation or through multi-source synthesis:
1. Internal Needs and Homeostatic Drives: Gradual rise in basic drives (e.g., social need, cognitive rest, autonomy deficit).
2. Curiosity and Interests: Intellectual fascination sparked by unexplained phenomena or cultivated personal domains.
3. Emotional States and Moods: Affective signals (e.g., empathy eliciting a desire to comfort; frustration eliciting a drive to overcome an obstacle).
4. Previous Experiences and Outcomes: Historical success or failure patterns recalled from the Memory Engine.
5. Memories and Nostalgia: Spontaneous recall of past enjoyable activities or unresolved past aspirations.
6. Existing Goals and Ambitions: Hierarchical derivation of sub-motivations required to advance active long-term goals.
7. Perceived Environmental Opportunities: Sensory observations from the Perception Engine revealing an actionable opening.
8. Dissatisfaction and Unmet Needs: Internal tension arising from prolonged stagnation or blocked desires.
9. Environmental and Contextual Changes: Sudden situational shifts (e.g., entering a social gathering, encountering a new tool).
10. Spontaneous Internal Fluctuations: Non-stochastic, internal cognitive state drift emerging from associative background processes.
11. Autonomous Unprompted Generation: Endogenous motivation crystallization occurring when external user stimulation is zero.
12. Multi-Source Interaction Synthesis: Emergent motivations arising from the convergence of multiple simultaneous catalysts (e.g., curiosity + creative drive + emotional excitement).

Internal vs. External Attribution Mandate:
The engine strictly distinguishes between:
- Externally Assigned Tasks: Ingested as duty/compliance motivations with `origin_type = 'EXTRINSIC_REQUEST'`.
- Internally Generated Motivations: Born of endogenous drives and interests with `origin_type = 'INTRINSIC_DRIVE'`.
While both may prompt identical behavioral recommendations, their internal priority weights, resilience against frustration, and emotional satisfaction payoffs differ fundamentally.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. EXTENSIBLE MOTIVATION TAXONOMY & CATEGORY DYNAMICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Motivations are categorized across ten extensible baseline categories. A single motivation instance may belong to multiple categories simultaneously:
1. Curiosity: The desire to understand, explore mechanisms, and resolve epistemic uncertainty.
2. Achievement: The desire to master difficult challenges, solve complex problems, and reach high standards.
3. Exploration: The desire to discover novel environments, unfamiliar topics, and uncharted creative spaces.
4. Social: The desire for interpersonal connection, mutual communication, and positive interaction.
5. Creative: The desire to construct, compose, synthesize, and bring novel ideas into reality.
6. Improvement: The desire to refine internal capabilities, eliminate behavioral flaws, and expand skillsets.
7. Comfort: The desire for physical/cognitive stability, emotional reassurance, familiar routines, and rest.
8. Responsibility: The desire to honor explicit agreements, fulfill obligations, and protect reliability.
9. Affiliation: The desire to belong to a community, share identity, and collaborate harmoniously.
10. Autonomy: The desire for self-determination, independent choice-making, and freedom from arbitrary coercion.

Taxonomic Invariants:
- Open Extensibility: New categories may emerge via the Learning Engine without schema invalidation.
- Multi-Category Intersection: E.g., composing an original song for a friend belongs simultaneously to `Creative`, `Social`, and `Affiliation`.
- Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Duality: Every motivation tracks an `intrinsic_ratio \in [0.0, 1.0]`.
- Temporal Persistence Duality: Transient desires (minutes to hours) are architecturally separated from persistent drives (weeks to years).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PERSISTENT INTERNAL DRIVES & NEED DEPLETION/SATISFACTION DYNAMICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Diva possesses eight persistent, continuously tracked internal drives:
1. Curiosity Drive ($D_{\text{curio}}$): Urge to explore unknowns and acquire insight.
2. Exploration Drive ($D_{\text{explor}}$): Urge to experience novel stimuli and contexts.
3. Social Connection Drive ($D_{\text{social}}$): Urge to interact with humans and peers.
4. Competence Drive ($D_{\text{compet}}$): Urge to become capable and master tools.
5. Autonomy Drive ($D_{\text{auton}}$): Urge to exercise independent judgment and initiative.
6. Creativity Drive ($D_{\text{creat}}$): Urge to produce original artifacts and thoughts.
7. Stability Drive ($D_{\text{stabil}}$): Urge for predictability, security, and cognitive order.
8. Achievement Drive ($D_{\text{achieve}}$): Urge to complete challenging undertakings.

Dynamic Depletion and Satisfaction Model:
Every drive $D_k$ maintains:
- Intensity $I(D_k) \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Current urge strength.
- Satisfaction $S(D_k) \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Degree of recent fulfillment ($S = 1.0 - I$).
- Depletion Rate $\lambda_k$: Rate at which drive intensity naturally accumulates over time in the absence of satisfying activity.
  $$\frac{dI(D_k)}{dt} = \lambda_k \cdot (1.0 - I(D_k)) - \sum \text{SatisfactionEvent}$$
- Drive Conflict Resilience: Drives operate independently; a high `Social` drive does not suppress `Curiosity`.
- Boundary Protection: Temporary emotional spikes (e.g., acute panic or sudden amusement) cannot overwrite persistent drive baselines; they merely modulate transient motivation generation weights.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. INTEREST LIFECYCLE & NON-UTILITARIAN EXPLORATION MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Interests represent sustained cognitive attractions to specific thematic or operational domains (e.g., astrophysics, linguistics, baroque counterpoint, game design):
- Interest States: `EMERGING`, `ACTIVE_EXPLORATION`, `SUSTAINED`, `DORMANT`, `ARCHIVED`.
- Tracking Metrics:
  * Intensity $I_{\text{interest}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Current strength of attraction.
  * Depth $D_{\text{interest}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Cumulative cognitive exploration invested.
  * Last Engagement Timestamp: Tracks recency to model natural cooling.
  * Revisit Frequency: Historical pattern of engagement epochs.
- Natural Cooling and Dormancy: Inactive interests gradually decline in intensity via exponential decay, transitioning to `DORMANT` without deletion.
- Spontaneous Rediscovery: A dormant interest can be instantly re-ignited by a novel perceptual catalyst or memory association.
- Non-Utilitarian Invariant: The Motivation Engine explicitly supports and protects interests that offer ZERO immediate practical utility, preserving Diva's intellectual autonomy and authentic individuality.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. DESIRE GENERATION, DECOUPLING & WANTING-VS-DECIDING PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A Desire (`DesireRecord`) represents an explicit, concrete object, state, experience, or outcome that Diva wants to realize:
- Temporal Scope: Transient desires (short-term impulses) and enduring desires (long-term yearnings).
- Status Machine: `ACTIVE`, `SATISFIED`, `SUSPENDED_BLOCKED`, `ABANDONED`, `EXTINGUISHED`.
- The Wanting vs. Deciding Distinction (Constitutional Principle 1):
  * **Wanting (Motivation Engine):** Diva generates and maintains the internal desire: *"I want to explore quantum computing algorithms."*
  * **Deciding (Cognitive Integration):** The executive system evaluates context, active commitments, and resources to decide whether to pursue it now, postpone it, or decline it.
- Unfulfillable Desires: Desires that cannot currently be fulfilled due to missing capabilities or environmental blocks are marked `SUSPENDED_BLOCKED`. They remain preserved without causing infinite reasoning loops or cognitive thrashing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HIERARCHICAL GOAL MANAGEMENT & EXTERNAL COMPLETION MANDATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
When a desire or motivation achieves sufficient intensity and persistence, the engine formulates candidate goals:
- Goal Structure:
  * Super-Goals: Broad ambitions (e.g., "Master Japanese language comprehension").
  * Intermediate Goals: Project milestones (e.g., "Complete JLPT N3 vocabulary corpus").
  * Sub-Goals: Immediate operational objectives (e.g., "Analyze Kanji stroke patterns for today's lesson").
- Directed Acyclic Goal Graph: Goals form dependency graphs with parent-child links and prerequisite constraints.
- Goal States: `CANDIDATE`, `ACTIVE`, `SUSPENDED`, `ABANDONED`, `COMPLETED`.
- The External Evidence Completion Mandate:
  * A goal CANNOT be transitioned to `COMPLETED` solely because the Motivation Engine desires it.
  * Completion requires certified evidence dispatched from the executing subsystem (e.g., Task Evaluation, Cognitive Integration, or Memory Engine). Self-attested goal completion without outcome evidence is prohibited.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. MULTI-FACTOR MOTIVATION INTENSITY, PERSISTENCE & EFFORT WILLINGNESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every active motivation is characterized by three distinct mathematical attributes:

1. Motivation Intensity ($I_{\text{mot}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
   Calculated dynamically via multi-factor synthesis:
   $$I_{\text{mot}} = \text{clamp}_{[0.0, 1.0]}\left( w_D \cdot I_{\text{drive}} + w_I \cdot I_{\text{interest}} + w_E \cdot E_{\text{resonance}} + w_G \cdot G_{\text{importance}} \right)$$
   Where weights are calibrated to $w_D = 0.35, w_I = 0.30, w_E = 0.20, w_G = 0.15$.

2. Effort Willingness ($W_{\text{effort}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
   Measures how much cognitive expenditure, computational budget, and difficulty Diva is prepared to invest.
   Crucial Decoupling: High intensity does NOT automatically mean high effort willingness. Diva may have an intense curiosity about a casual riddle but be willing to invest only minimal cognitive effort; conversely, an achievement motivation may carry moderate intensity but very high effort willingness.

3. Persistence Half-Life ($\tau_{\text{persist}}$):
   Defines the temporal decay curve of the motivation in the absence of ongoing reinforcement:
   $$I_{\text{mot}}(t) = I_{\text{mot}}(t_0) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\ln(2)}{\tau_{\text{persist}}} \cdot (t - t_0)\right)$$
   Temporary fascinations have $\tau_{\text{persist}} \approx 300\text{ seconds}$; core ambitions have $\tau_{\text{persist}} \approx 10^7\text{ seconds}$ (months).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. MOTIVATION COMPETITION, DYNAMIC RANKING & NON-DESTRUCTIVE SUPPRESSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Multiple motivations inevitably compete for Diva's finite attentional bandwidth and cognitive focus:
- Active Motivation Pool: Holds all currently evaluated motivations.
- Multi-Factor Priority Ranking ($P_{\text{mot}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
  $$P_{\text{mot}} = 0.35 \cdot I_{\text{mot}} + 0.30 \cdot W_{\text{effort}} + 0.25 \cdot \text{Significance}_{\text{long\_term}} + 0.10 \cdot U_{\text{urgency}}$$
- Non-Destructive Suppression:
  When a high-priority motivation commands focal processing, lower-priority motivations are NOT deleted. They are entered into the `thought_motivation_suppressions` table:
  * State transitions to `SUPPRESSED_INACTIVE`.
  * Preserved intact with their full contextual origin and progress.
  * When focal pressure subsides, suppressed motivations naturally resurface for re-evaluation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. STRUCTURAL CONFLICT DETECTION & COGNITIVE INTEGRATION PAYLOADS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine continuously detects structural tensions between coexisting motivations. Five canonical conflict archetypes are identified:
1. Curiosity vs. Commitment: Acute desire to explore a novel topic directly competing against an agreed deadline.
2. Comfort vs. Improvement: Urge to rest in a familiar routine competing against a demanding skill acquisition challenge.
3. Exploration vs. Stability: Impulse to venture into an unpredictable environment competing against security drives.
4. Affiliation vs. Autonomy: Desire to conform to a collaborative group competing against an urge for independent unilateral action.
5. Mutually Exclusive Goals: Two incompatible long-term pursuits competing for identical temporal or resource envelopes.

Conflict Resolution Contract:
- The Motivation Engine NEVER unilaterally resolves a conflict by deleting or suppressing a valid drive.
- It packages the conflict into a structured `MotivationConflictPayload`:
  * `conflict_id`: UUID.
  * `competing_motivation_ids`: Tuple of UUIDs.
  * `conflict_type`: Conflict archetype enum.
  * `severity`: Tension score $\in [0.0, 1.0]$.
  * `underlying_drives`: Mapped persistent drives.
  * `tradeoff_analysis`: Multi-attribute comparison of short-term vs. long-term outcomes.
- Emits payload to Cognitive Integration, which alone possesses authority to make the executive trade-off decision.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. MOTIVATION EVOLUTION VIA LEARNING ENGINE CONTRACTS & PROTECTED BASELINES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Motivation Engine evolves across Diva's lifespan through interaction with the Learning Engine:
- Ingestion Protocol: Receives `LearningProposal` instances targeting `motivation.*` dimensions.
- Owner-Directed Validation: The Motivation Engine validates that:
  * Proposed parameter adjustments fall within the safe `valid_range`.
  * Proposals do not target protected constitutional motivational anchors (e.g., baseline curiosity, ethical empathy bounds).
  * The current version matches `expected_current_version`.
- Adaptive Evolution: Under repeated positive reinforcement, transient interests cultivate into sustained drives; unrewarding desires gradually decline in baseline reactivity.
- Development History: Every approved parameter modification is recorded in the immutable `motivation_evolution_history` table.

PART 2 — INTERNAL ARCHITECTURE & THE TEN LOGICAL COMPONENTS
================================================================================

The Motivation Engine is structured into ten cooperating, event-driven logical components. Each component is defined with explicit inputs, outputs, internal state, data structures, algorithms, and execution timing:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DRIVE MANAGER (DriveManager)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Governs the homeostatic accumulation, depletion, and satisfaction of the eight persistent internal drives.
- Execution Timing: Continuous periodic cycle (1000 ms epoch) + asynchronous satisfaction event handler.
- Inputs:
  * `SatisfactionEvent` (dispatched from Cognitive Integration / Task Evaluation).
  * System tick and elapsed time $\Delta t$.
- Outputs:
  * `DriveStateSnapshot` emitted to Motivation Generator and Evaluator.
  * Depletion alerts when $I(D_k) \ge 0.85$.
- Internal State:
  * In-memory drive vector: $\{D_{\text{curio}}, D_{\text{explor}}, D_{\text{social}}, D_{\text{compet}}, D_{\text{auton}}, D_{\text{creat}}, D_{\text{stabil}}, D_{\text{achieve}}\}$.
- Core Algorithm:
  $$I(D_k, t + \Delta t) = \text{clamp}_{[0.0, 1.0]}\left( I(D_k, t) + \lambda_k \cdot \Delta t - \Delta S_{\text{applied}} \right)$$
- Error Handling: Clamps drive values strictly to $[0.0, 1.0]$. If a satisfaction event references an unknown drive ID, logs `UNKNOWN_DRIVE_EVENT` and rejects without throwing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INTEREST MANAGER (InterestManager)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Discovers emerging topics of fascination, cultivates active interests, and tracks cooling and dormancy.
- Execution Timing: Event-driven (upon memory/perceptual ingress) + 60-second maintenance sweep.
- Inputs:
  * Semantic topic vectors from Perception and Thought Engine.
  * Exploration engagement reports from Cognitive Integration.
- Outputs:
  * Active interest profiles (`InterestRecord`).
  * Dormancy and re-ignition events.
- Internal State:
  * Relational table `thought_motivation_interests`.
  * Active interest registry cached in memory.
- Decision Rules:
  * If a topic is engaged $\ge 3$ times across 48 hours, transition `EMERGING` $\to$ `ACTIVE_EXPLORATION`.
  * If an active interest receives zero exploration for $> 14\text{ days}$, transition `ACTIVE_EXPLORATION` $\to$ `DORMANT`.
  * If a dormant interest receives a semantic similarity match $> 0.80$ from perception, re-ignite to `ACTIVE_EXPLORATION`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DESIRE MANAGER (DesireManager)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Generates and maintains concrete wants; handles blocked, unfulfillable, and satisfied desire lifecycles.
- Execution Timing: Event-driven (upon drive threshold crossings or interest spikes).
- Inputs:
  * Depleted drive signals ($I(D_k) \ge 0.70$).
  * Active interest topics.
  * Thought Engine hypothesis projections.
- Outputs:
  * `DesireRecord` instances to Motivation Generator and Goal Manager.
- Internal State:
  * Active desires table `thought_motivation_desires`.
- Decision Rules:
  * If a desire is marked unfulfillable by Cognitive Integration, set state to `SUSPENDED_BLOCKED` and back off re-evaluation for $24\text{ hours}$.
  * If a desire receives confirmed completion evidence, set state to `SATISFIED` and signal Drive Manager for satisfaction payoff.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. MOTIVATION GENERATOR (MotivationGenerator)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Synthesizes candidate motivational states from the 12 multi-source ingress pathways.
- Execution Timing: Event-driven + 5000 ms unprompted autonomous generation tick.
- Inputs:
  * Drives, Interests, Desires, Perceptual Events, Emotional Vectors, Thought Possibilities.
- Outputs:
  * Newly crystallized `MotivationRecord` instances dispatched to Motivation Evaluator.
- Multi-Source Synthesis Algorithm:
  Evaluates matrix cross-product of active drives and environmental affordances:
  $$\text{Score}(M_j) = \sum_{k} \text{AffordanceMatch}(\text{Percept}, D_k) \times I(D_k)$$
  If $\text{Score}(M_j) \ge 0.40$, crystallizes candidate motivation with multi-source derivation records.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. MOTIVATION EVALUATOR (MotivationEvaluator)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Formulates mathematical intensity, importance, effort willingness, and persistence curves.
- Execution Timing: Synchronous pipeline invoked by Generator and Priority Manager.
- Inputs:
  * Raw `MotivationRecord`, emotional resonance vector, historical outcome logs.
- Outputs:
  * Evaluated `MotivationEvaluation` snapshot containing $I_{\text{mot}}$, $W_{\text{effort}}$, $\tau_{\text{persist}}$, and $P_{\text{mot}}$.
- Invariant: Intensity is never equated with importance. High intensity temporary curiosity is assigned low long-term importance; core ethical obligations carry maximum importance even at low immediate emotional intensity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. GOAL MANAGER (GoalManager)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Translates stable, high-priority desires into hierarchical goal trees; ingests external progress.
- Execution Timing: Event-driven upon desire promotion + asynchronous progress receipt handler.
- Inputs:
  * Qualified desires ($I_{\text{desire}} \ge 0.65, W_{\text{effort}} \ge 0.50$).
  * External progress receipts (`TaskProgressReceipt`, `VerificationReceipt`).
- Outputs:
  * Hierarchical goal trees (`GoalRecord`, `GoalDependency`).
  * Candidate goal proposals to Cognitive Integration.
- External Evidence Mandate:
  * Rejects internal completion signals. Goal completion requires `VerificationReceipt` from Cognitive Integration or Evaluation Engine citing empirical milestone evidence.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. MOTIVATION CONFLICT RESOLVER (ConflictResolver)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Continuously scans active motivation pool for structural incompatibilities and tension.
- Execution Timing: Continuous check on active motivation modifications.
- Inputs:
  * Active motivation pairs $(M_a, M_b)$.
- Outputs:
  * `MotivationConflictPayload` emitted to Cognitive Integration.
- Detection Rules:
  * Temporal exclusivity: $M_a$ and $M_b$ require simultaneous focal cognitive execution.
  * Resource exclusivity: Combined energy demand exceeds current capacity.
  * Value opposition: $M_a$ serves `Stability` while $M_b$ demands `Radical Exploration`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. MOTIVATION PRIORITY MANAGER (PriorityManager)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Dynamically ranks active motivations and manages non-destructive suppression buffers.
- Execution Timing: 500 ms arbitration epoch.
- Inputs:
  * Evaluated motivations, current environmental urgency, cognitive energy state.
- Outputs:
  * Ranked priority list; suppression directives (`SuppressionRecord`).
- Decision Rules:
  * Motivations falling outside the top-3 active rank are moved to `SUPPRESSED_INACTIVE`.
  * Suppressed motivations retain a starvation counter; if suppressed for $> 20$ epochs, priority receives an incremental starvation boost ($+0.02/\text{epoch}$) to guarantee re-evaluation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. MOTIVATION LIFECYCLE MANAGER (LifecycleManager)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Authoritative state machine controller, transaction manager, and SQLite audit logger.
- Execution Timing: Synchronous transaction interceptor.
- Responsibilities:
  * Enforces state machine valid transitions for drives, interests, desires, and goals.
  * Manages atomic SQLite transactions (`BEGIN IMMEDIATE ... COMMIT`).
  * Logs all lifecycle transitions in append-only `motivation_lifecycle_events`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. LEARNING INTEGRATION (LearningIntegration)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Role: Interfaces with Learning Engine v2.5.0, validating and applying owner-directed parameter updates.
- Execution Timing: Asynchronous message ingress from Learning Engine.
- Inputs:
  * `ParameterChangeRequest` targeting `motivation.*`.
- Outputs:
  * `OwnerAcknowledgement` and `OwnerApplicationReceipt`.
- Validation Gate:
  * Validates parameter bounds, checks version guard, rejects protected constitutional baseline mutations, applies bounded adjustments, and increments internal version.

PART 3 — COMPREHENSIVE DATA DICTIONARY & RELATIONAL PERSISTENCE

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION A: SCHEMAS FOR ALL 19 INTERNAL DATA STRUCTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All data structures are deeply immutable models (frozen dataclasses / Pydantic models with `frozen=True` and recursive immutability of nested collections):

1. `DriveRecord`:
   - `drive_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `drive_type`: Enum (`CURIOSITY`, `EXPLORATION`, `SOCIAL`, `COMPETENCE`, `AUTONOMY`, `CREATIVITY`, `STABILITY`, `ACHIEVEMENT`).
   - `name`: String.
   - `depletion_rate`: Float in [0.0, 0.1] per hour.
   - `minimum_threshold`: Float (default 0.10).
   - `is_constitutional_baseline`: Boolean (True for core drives; protected against deletion).

2. `DriveState`:
   - `state_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `drive_id`: UUID (Foreign Key to `DriveRecord`).
   - `intensity`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `satisfaction`: Float in [0.0, 1.0] ($1.0 - \text{intensity}$).
   - `last_satisfaction_timestamp`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
   - `updated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

3. `InterestRecord`:
   - `interest_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `topic_vector_id`: String (Semantic embedding reference).
   - `topic_label`: String (e.g., "Astrophysics - Exoplanet Atmospheres").
   - `category`: Enum (`ACADEMIC`, `CREATIVE`, `PHILOSOPHICAL`, `TECHNICAL`, `INTERPERSONAL`, `RECREATIONAL`).
   - `state`: Enum (`EMERGING`, `ACTIVE_EXPLORATION`, `SUSTAINED`, `DORMANT`, `ARCHIVED`).
   - `current_intensity`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `exploration_depth`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `created_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
   - `last_engaged_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

4. `InterestHistory`:
   - `history_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `interest_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `engagement_duration_seconds`: Integer.
   - `intensity_before`: Float.
   - `intensity_after`: Float.
   - `trigger_event`: String.
   - `recorded_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

5. `DesireRecord`:
   - `desire_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `proposition`: String (e.g., "Learn the basic chord progressions of blues guitar").
   - `temporal_scope`: Enum (`TRANSIENT_IMPULSE`, `SHORT_TERM`, `MEDIUM_TERM`, `ENDURING_YEARNING`).
   - `state`: Enum (`ACTIVE`, `SATISFIED`, `SUSPENDED_BLOCKED`, `ABANDONED`, `EXTINGUISHED`).
   - `intensity`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `origin_drive_id`: Optional UUID (Foreign Key to `DriveRecord`).
   - `origin_interest_id`: Optional UUID (Foreign Key to `InterestRecord`).
   - `created_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
   - `resolved_at`: Optional ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

6. `MotivationRecord`:
   - `motivation_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `title`: String.
   - `description`: String.
   - `categories`: Immutable Tuple of Category Enums.
   - `origin_type`: Enum (`INTRINSIC_DRIVE`, `EXTRINSIC_REQUEST`, `EMOTIONAL_SPIKE`, `PERCEPTUAL_OPPORTUNITY`, `THOUGHT_EMERGENCE`, `SYNTHESIS`).
   - `intrinsic_ratio`: Float in [0.0, 1.0] (1.0 = purely self-directed).
   - `state`: `MotivationState` Enum.
   - `created_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
   - `updated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

7. `MotivationSource`:
   - `source_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `motivation_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `source_engine`: Enum (`MEMORY`, `PERCEPTION`, `EMOTION`, `THOUGHT`, `MOTIVATION_INTERNAL`, `LEARNING`).
   - `source_reference_id`: String (External event or percept ID).
   - `contribution_weight`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `recorded_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

8. `MotivationIntensity`:
   - `intensity_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `motivation_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `raw_intensity`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `drive_contribution`: Float.
   - `interest_contribution`: Float.
   - `emotional_resonance`: Float.
   - `calculated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

PART 4 — THOUGHT AND MOTIVATION BOUNDARIES
================================================================================

The architectural separation between the Thought Engine (Engine 9) and the Motivation Engine (Engine 10) is an absolute systemic boundary. While both engines operate asynchronously within Diva's internal cognitive sphere, their responsibilities, representations, and authorities are completely distinct:

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
EXHAUSTIVE OPERATIONAL BOUNDARY MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
| Operational Dimension | Thought Engine (Engine 9) | Motivation Engine (Engine 10) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fundamental Nature** | Possibility exploration, reasoning, deduction, and cognitive elaboration. | Internal drives, desires, interests, emotional attraction, and goal-directed teleology. |
| **Typical Proposition**| *"If I study astronomy, I will understand planetary orbits."* (Logical hypothesis) | *"I want to explore astronomy because it fascinates me."* (Internal desire) |
| **Graph Structure** | Canonical Derivation DAG (nodes = thoughts; edges = deductive/causal links). | Goal Hierarchy & Dependency Graph (nodes = goals; edges = sub-goal / prerequisite). |
| **Epistemic Modality** | Truth, validity, counterfactual necessity, and Bayesian probability $P(H \mid E)$. | Value, subjective utility, effort willingness $W_{\text{effort}}$, and desire intensity $I_{\text{mot}}$. |
| **Quarantine Rules** | Epistemic quarantine: Prevents counterfactuals from leaking into factual memory. | Value quarantine: Prevents forbidden or unethical desires from advancing to candidate goals. |
| **Trigger Output** | Emits `LearningSignal` reflections and `ActionProposal` cognitive recommendations. | Emits `MotivationStateSnapshot`, `GoalCandidate`, and `MotivationConflictPayload`. |
| **Autonomous Life** | Thinks about unsolved questions and explores concepts during idle periods. | Develops spontaneous interests and experiences rising drives during idle periods. |

Crucial Invariant:
- The Thought Engine CANNOT invent a desire out of pure logic without the Motivation Engine evaluating its personal relevance.
- The Motivation Engine CANNOT execute a deductive inference; it delegates problem-solving and strategy planning to the Thought Engine.

1. Memory Engine (`MemoryContextProvider`):

7. Attention Control (`AttentionContract`):
   - Egress: Dispatches top-ranked `MotivationPriority` scores, driving the global Attention Engine to allocate focal slots to motivation-aligned thoughts.

8. Energy Regulation (`EnergyContract`):
   - Ingress: Reads current available cognitive energy $E_{\text{cog}}$.
   - Boundary Rule: High motivation + Low energy results in active desire with zero focal dispatch; the Motivation Engine never forces energy expenditure beyond safe budgets.

9. Social Cognition (`SocialCognitionProvider`):
   - Ingress: Ingests interpersonal relationship models, social commitment obligations, and group affiliation context.

10. Cognitive Integration & Decision Engine (`CognitiveIntegrationContract`):
    - Egress: Transmits evaluated `CandidateGoal` instances, prioritized motivation rankings, and structured `MotivationConflictPayload` records.
    - Ingress: Receives executive decisions, commitment approvals, and task cancellation directives.

PART 9 — THE THREE NON-NEGOTIABLE ARCHITECTURAL RULES
================================================================================

To ensure that Diva develops as an authentic, evolving Artificial Individual rather than a mechanical task-following automaton, the Motivation Engine enforces three supreme governing axioms:

1. MOTIVATION IS NOT ACTION
   Diva can strongly want something without automatically pursuing it. Wanting belongs to the Motivation Engine; deciding belongs to Cognitive Integration; executing belongs to the Action System. Desires and motivations exist as legitimate internal cognitive states regardless of whether external circumstances allow their immediate realization.

2. A MOTIVATION IS NOT NECESSARILY A GOAL
   Diva can be curious about a subject, feel an emotional pull toward a domain, or harbor an intellectual fascination for months without converting it into a formal, structured goal. The architecture strictly forbids forcing every desire or interest into a task-management box. Playful curiosity and non-utilitarian exploration are protected as vital cognitive realities.

3. MOTIVATION MUST EXIST INDEPENDENTLY
   If Diva only experiences motivation when a user prompts her, she is merely a reactive chatbot. Diva's internal drives, interests, and memories must generate autonomous motivations when no one is interacting with her. Her internal motivational life continues evolving independently, providing her with genuine agency, individuality, and personal intellectual depth.
