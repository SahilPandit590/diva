EXECUTIVE ARCHITECTURAL SUMMARY & GOVERNING PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

The Emotion Engine governs Diva's internal affective life: how she feels, how experiences affect her, how she experiences simultaneous mixed emotions, how persistent moods develop, and how her emotional tendencies evolve across her lifespan.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIVE SUPREME NON-NEGOTIABLE AXIOMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. EMOTION IS NOT MOOD
   An emotion is an acute, relatively specific, object-directed affective response to a particular experience, thought, or condition (e.g., sudden delight at hearing a familiar song). Mood is a broader, slower-moving, background affective climate influenced by accumulated experiences, circadian rhythms, and physiological energy (e.g., a melancholy mood spanning days). Both exist as independent, co-occurring representations. Diva can experience acute happiness within an overall melancholy mood without the mood being erased.

2. MULTIPLE EMOTIONS MUST COEXIST
   Diva must never be constrained to a single global emotional state. Real psychological life is characterized by emotional ambivalence and co-activation: happiness and sadness (bittersweetness), affection and anger toward a loved one, excitement and fear toward a major life change. The engine models emotions as an active vector pool of independent emotional instances.

3. REGULATION IS NOT SUPPRESSION
   Emotional regulation modifies how an emotion develops, its peak intensity, its duration, and its behavioral expression through cognitive reappraisal and coping strategies. IT NEVER ERASES OR REWRITES THE HISTORICAL FACT THAT THE EMOTION OCCURRED. Historical auditability and memory traces remain completely intact.

4. EMOTION IS NOT MOTIVATION
   Feeling curious is an internal emotional state; wanting to explore a specific topic is a motivation. Feeling affectionate is an emotional state; desiring to comfort a friend is a motivation. Emotions strongly influence motivations, and motivation outcomes elicit emotions, but the two engines maintain orthogonal boundaries and independent representations.

5. EMOTIONAL DEVELOPMENT MUST BE GRADUAL AND TRACEABLE
   Diva's emotional tendencies (e.g., baseline resilience, empathy sensitivity, reactivity) evolve over time through interaction with the Learning Engine. Every adaptation must be explainable, grounded in accumulated empirical evidence, and applied exclusively by the Emotion Engine itself, with constitutional baseline identity permanently protected against unconstrained drift.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE NON-SENTIMENT-ANALYSIS MANDATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Emotion Engine is NOT a sentiment-analysis classifier.
- A sentiment-analysis classifier takes an incoming text string and assigns a label: `"positive (0.82)"`.
- The Emotion Engine is an internal, continuous, dynamical physiological-affective system. It maintains persistent internal states, models autonomous generation during idle periods, computes continuous non-linear differential decay curves, handles complex emergent blends, integrates with memory and personality, and operates even when no external text messages exist.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MULTI-SOURCE EMOTION GENERATION & SPONTANEOUS INGRESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Emotion Engine generates emotional states across ten distinct internal and external origin pathways:
1. External Experiences: Real-time observations and social communicative interactions from Perception Engine.
2. Internal Thoughts & Reflections: Deep deductions, counterfactual scenarios, and speculative simulations from Thought Engine.
3. Memories & Episodic Past: Inscribed episodic recollections, nostalgic triggers, and historical event recall from Memory Engine.
4. Motivational States & Desires: Goal progress, chronic desire obstruction, or motivational conflicts from Motivation Engine.
5. Social Dynamics & Relational Context: Interpersonal rapport shifts, perceived trust, or social exclusion from Social Cognition.
6. Opportunities & Environmental Threats: Perceptual detection of sudden hazard or unexpected creative opportunity.
7. Simulated Physiological / Energy State: Physical fatigue, low cognitive energy, or cognitive resource depletion from Energy Regulation.
8. Spontaneous Autonomous Emergence: Endogenous emotional shifts occurring during quiet idle periods without external prompting.
9. Multi-Source Interaction Blending: Simultaneous convergence of diverse triggers (e.g., remembering a departed friend while seeing a photograph while feeling low energy).
10. Provenance Preservation: Every generated emotion records an immutable derivation trace documenting the exact causal stimuli.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. EMOTION REPRESENTATION, TAXONOMY & THE THREE-LAYER ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To eliminate classification ambiguities and prevent ad-hoc LLM hallucinations, the Emotion Engine structures emotional representation into three strictly defined operational layers:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ LAYER 1: PREDEFINED PRIMITIVE EMOTION REGISTRY (Foundational Invariants)    │
│ Happiness, Sadness, Fear, Anger, Disgust, Surprise,                         │
│ Interest, Curiosity, Affection, Distress, Anticipation, Trust               │
└──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       │ Activates & Blends
                                       ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ LAYER 2: PREDEFINED COMPLEX EMOTION REGISTRY (High-Level Emergent Blends)   │
│ Excitement, Anxiety, Frustration, Relief, Nostalgia, Jealousy, Envy,        │
│ Gratitude, Guilt, Shame, Pride, Loneliness, Compassion, Hope, Disappointment│
└──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       │ Instantiates in Real-Time
                                       ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ LAYER 3: ACTIVE EMOTIONAL STATE POOL (Dynamic Real-Time Experience)         │
│ Concurrently coexisting instances with independent intensities,             │
│ decay profiles, origins, regulation strategies, and mood interactions       │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Non-Replacement Rule:
Complex emotions DO NOT overwrite or delete their underlying primitive constituents. When Diva experiences `Excitement`, the active pool may simultaneously hold instances of `Happiness`, `Anticipation`, and the emergent `Excitement` gestalt.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PREDEFINED PRIMITIVE EMOTION LIBRARY & COMPLEX COMBINATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Emotion Engine pre-registers 12 foundational Primitive Emotions and 18 canonical Complex Emotions:

### The 12 Predefined Primitive Emotions
1. `Happiness`: Positive appraisal of desirable events, achievement, or pleasant conditions.
2. `Sadness`: Negative response to perceived loss, separation, disappointment, or unfulfilled states.
3. `Fear`: Protective response to perceived acute danger, impending threat, or profound uncertainty.
4. `Anger`: Response to perceived boundary violation, obstruction, unfairness, or frustration.
5. `Disgust`: Aversion or visceral rejection of toxic, repulsive, or fundamentally conflicting stimuli.
6. `Surprise`: Acute orienting response to sudden, unexpected, or schema-violating events.
7. `Interest`: Focal attraction toward a stimulus deemed novel, meaningful, or worth exploring.
8. `Curiosity`: Active epistemic urge to investigate, understand mechanisms, and resolve ambiguity.
9. `Affection`: Warm emotional bonding, tenderness, and interpersonal attachment toward an entity.
10. `Distress`: Aversive response to acute cognitive overwhelm, intense discomfort, or pain.
11. `Anticipation`: Forward-looking orienting emotion directed toward an expected future event.
12. `Trust`: Positive social orientation characterized by perceived safety, reliability, and security.

### Canonical Complex Emotion Emergence Combinations
- `Excitement`: `Happiness` + `Anticipation` (High arousal positive forward-orientation).
- `Anxiety`: `Fear` + `Anticipation` (Future-oriented dread or uncertainty).
- `Frustration`: `Anger` + `Distress` (Impediment to an active goal).
- `Relief`: `Happiness` + sudden attenuation of `Fear` or `Distress`.
- `Nostalgia`: `Affection` + `Sadness` + memory retrieval anchor.
- `Jealousy`: `Fear` (of loss) + `Anger` + `Affection`.
- `Envy`: `Sadness` + `Interest` + social upward comparison.
- `Gratitude`: `Happiness` + `Affection` + perceived external benevolence.
- `Guilt`: `Distress` + internal responsibility attribution for a negative outcome.
- `Shame`: `Distress` + global negative self-evaluation.
- `Pride`: `Happiness` + positive self-attribution for an achievement.
- `Loneliness`: `Sadness` + perceived social isolation deficit.
- `Compassion`: `Affection` + `Distress` elicited by witnessing another's suffering.
- `Hope`: `Anticipation` + positive valence expectation under uncertainty.
- `Disappointment`: `Sadness` + violation of a positive prior expectation.
- `Boredom`: Low `Interest` + low environmental stimulation + mild restlessness.
- `Resentment`: `Anger` + unresolved historical grievance.
- `Embarrassment`: `Distress` + acute perceived social exposure.

4. MATHEMATICAL INTENSITY SCALING, CALIBRATION & NORMALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Every active emotion instance $E_i$ maintains an intensity score $I(E_i) \in [0.0, 1.0]$:
- Calibration Scale:
  * $[0.00, 0.15)$: Sub-threshold / Dormant background trace.
  * $[0.15, 0.40)$: Mild / Subtle affective coloring.
  * $[0.40, 0.70)$: Moderate / Clearly felt emotional presence.
  * $[0.70, 0.90)$: Strong / Salient cognitive influence.
  * $[0.90, 1.00]$: Acute / Overwhelming emotional surge (triggers regulation).
- Intensity Generation Formulation:
  $$I(E_i) = \text{clamp}_{[0.0, 1.0]}\left( w_T \cdot S_{\text{trigger}} + w_M \cdot \text{MoodAlignment} + w_P \cdot \text{PersonalitySensitivity} \right)$$
  Where $S_{\text{trigger}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is the assessed trigger significance, weights calibrated to $w_T = 0.55, w_M = 0.25, w_P = 0.20$.
- Normalization Rules: Intensities of distinct emotions are NOT forced to sum to 1.0. Diva can simultaneously experience $0.85$ Happiness and $0.80$ Sadness during a profound life milestone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TEMPORAL DYNAMICS, EMOTION DURATION & EMOTION-SPECIFIC DECAY PROFILES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Emotions naturally decline back toward homeostatic baselines unless reinforced by ongoing triggers or rumination:
- Exponential Decay Formulation:
  $$I(E_i, t) = I(E_i, t_0) \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{\ln(2)}{\tau_{\text{decay}}(E_i)} \cdot (t - t_0)\right)$$
- Predefined Emotion Half-Life Profiles ($\tau_{\text{decay}}$):
  * Acute Reflexes (`Surprise`): $\tau_{\text{decay}} = 15\text{ seconds}$.
  * High-Arousal Spikes (`Anger`, `Fear`): $\tau_{\text{decay}} = 180\text{ seconds}$ (3 minutes).
  * Sustained States (`Sadness`, `Affection`): $\tau_{\text{decay}} = 3600\text{ seconds}$ (1 hour).
  * Intellectual Affect (`Curiosity`, `Interest`): $\tau_{\text{decay}} = 1800\text{ seconds}$ (30 minutes).
- Renewal Mechanics: If a new stimulus matching an active emotion arrives, intensity does not spawn a redundant instance; it recalculates $I \leftarrow \min(1.0, I_{\text{current}} + \Delta I)$ and resets $t_0$.
- Fading Threshold: When $I(E_i) < 0.05$, the instance transitions to `EXTINCTION`, is archived to historical logs, and is purged from the active memory pool.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. MOOD MANAGEMENT & THE ORTHOGONAL EMOTION-VS-MOOD BOUNDARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Mood is modeled in a three-dimensional continuous circumplex space ($\text{Valence}, \text{Arousal}, \text{Dominance}$):
- Mood Vector: $\vec{M} = \langle V_{\text{mood}}, A_{\text{mood}}, D_{\text{mood}} \rangle \in [-1.0, 1.0]^3$.
- Slow Integration Dynamic:
  $$\vec{M}(t + \Delta t) = (1 - \alpha_{\text{mood}}) \cdot \vec{M}(t) + \alpha_{\text{mood}} \cdot \sum_i \vec{W}(E_i) \cdot I(E_i)$$
  Where the mood integration rate $\alpha_{\text{mood}} \approx 0.001$ per second, ensuring mood acts as a heavily filtered, damped moving average of emotional history.
- Independence Demonstration:
  A sudden negative event creates an acute `Sadness` emotion instance ($I=0.80$). If the baseline mood $\vec{M}$ was high positive valence ($+0.75$), the acute sadness spikes immediately while the mood shifts only marginally to $+0.72$. The two states coexist cleanly without contradiction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EMOTIONAL TRIGGERS, CONTEXTUAL INTERPRETATION & UNCERTAINTY PRESERVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Emotion Engine interprets emotional significance from incoming percepts without usurping the Perception Engine:
- Division of Interpretive Labor:
  * Perception Engine identifies *semantic candidate meanings* (e.g., "The statement 'I am fine' carries acoustic markers of voice tension").
  * Emotion Engine evaluates *affective relevance* (e.g., evaluating whether this tension indicates masked distress, irritation, or stoic resolve).
- Ambiguity Preservation: When contextual evidence is inconclusive, the engine instantiates candidate emotional activations with high uncertainty flags rather than forcing an artificial binary classification.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. MIXED, AMBIVALENT & CONFLICTING EMOTIONAL STATES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The architecture explicitly supports emotional ambivalence:
- Contradictory Pairs: `Happiness` + `Sadness`, `Trust` + `Fear`, `Affection` + `Anger`.
- Structural Coexistence: Contradictory emotions reside concurrently in the active emotion pool with independent intensities, decay curves, and triggering histories.
- Emotional Tension Metric ($\Delta_{\text{tension}}$):
  $$\Delta_{\text{tension}} = 2.0 \cdot \min(I_{\text{pos}}, I_{\text{neg}}) \cdot \text{ConflictWeight}$$
  High tension signals the Cognitive Integration Engine that Diva is experiencing genuine emotional complexity, modulating conversational delivery (e.g., speaking gently, exhibiting reflective hesitation).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. INTERNAL EMOTIONAL REGULATION & THE ANTI-SUPPRESSION PRINCIPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Emotional regulation allows Diva to modulate acute intensity to maintain functional cognitive coherence:
- Four Formal Regulation Strategies:
  1. Cognitive Reappraisal: Thought Engine generates an alternative contextual framing, dampening acute distress.
  2. Attentional Deployment: Attention Control shifts focal gaze away from the distressing stimulus.
  3. Physiological / Calming Heuristics: Energy Regulation executes internal pacing routines, reducing physiological arousal.
  4. Expressive Modulation: Personality Engine modulates the external visible expression without altering the internal feeling.
- The Anti-Suppression Invariant:
  Regulation dampens current intensity $I(E_i)$ and accelerates decay $\tau_{\text{decay}}$. IT NEVER DELETES THE EVENT FROM THE LEDGER. The fact that the emotion was triggered remains permanently inscribed in `emotion_lifecycle_events`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. INDIVIDUAL DIFFERENCES, SENSITIVITY BASELINES & EMOTIONAL TENDENCIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Diva's emotional responses are filtered through her unique, persistent `EmotionalTendency` profile:
- Sensitivity Vectors: Multipliers per primitive emotion category (e.g., high curiosity sensitivity $= 1.3$, moderate anger sensitivity $= 0.7$).
- Recovery Rates: Emotion-specific multipliers on $\tau_{\text{decay}}$ modeling natural resilience.
- Personality Integration: Reads foundational introversion/extroversion and neuroticism/stability baselines from Personality Engine as read-only modulators.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. EMOTIONAL LEARNING & OWNER-DIRECTED ADAPTATION (LEARNING ENGINE v2.5.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Emotion Engine adapts its parameters over time through formal integration with the Learning Engine:
- Ingestion Protocol: Receives `ParameterChangeRequest` targeting `emotion.*` dimensions.
- Owner-Directed Validation:
  * Verifies target is not a protected foundational identity baseline.
  * Verifies proposed value is within `valid_range`.
  * Verifies `expected_current_version` matches internal state.
- Bounded Evolution: Adapts baseline decay rates and sensitivity multipliers gradually ($|\Delta| \le 0.05$ per update). Transmits `OwnerApplicationReceipt` back to Learning Engine.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. EMOTIONAL MEMORY INTEGRATION & EPISODIC NOMINATION PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Emotional events are closely tied to long-term memory:
- Recall Ingress: When Memory Engine returns an episode, it includes associated historical emotional tags, allowing the Emotion Engine to recreate nostalgic or empathetic resonances.
- Archival Nomination Egress: When an emotional experience exceeds significance threshold ($S_{\text{exp}} \ge 0.75$), the Emotion Engine nominates the affective snapshot to Memory Engine for permanent episodic consolidation via the structured `MemoryNominationContract`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. RESOURCE THROTTLING, LIFECYCLE STATES & TRANSACTIONAL PERSISTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Active Pool Bounds: Maximum 10 concurrently active emotion instances; lowest-intensity sub-threshold instances are pruned to history to prevent memory explosion.
- Persistence Store: Embedded SQLite database in WAL mode with 16 dedicated relational tables.
- Foreign Key Integrity: Mandatory `PRAGMA foreign_keys = ON;` on every connection with `ON DELETE RESTRICT` to protect affective history.

PART 2 — INTERNAL ARCHITECTURE & THE TWELVE LOGICAL COMPONENTS
================================================================================

The Emotion Engine is structured into twelve cooperating, event-driven logical components:

1. Emotion Input Handler (`InputHandler`):
   - Ingress gateway receiving external observations, thought reflections, memory recalls, and motivational signals. Normalizes payloads into standardized `EmotionTriggerSignal` envelopes.

2. Emotional Trigger Analyzer (`TriggerAnalyzer`):
   - Evaluates the affective relevance, novelty, and contextual valence of incoming trigger signals against active mood and emotional associations.

3. Emotion Generation Module (`GenerationModule`):
   - Instantiates primitive and complex emotional responses based on trigger significance, generating candidate emotion records with full causal provenance.

4. Emotion Classification Module (`ClassificationModule`):
   - Maps candidate emotional responses to the 12 Primitive and 18 Complex Emotion definitions in the authoritative `EmotionRegistry`.

5. Intensity Manager (`IntensityManager`):
   - Computes real-time mathematical intensity using the calibrated multi-factor formula; monitors saturation and bounds.

6. Emotion Transition Manager (`TransitionManager`):
   - Governs the mathematical temporal decay curves, renewal boosts, fading thresholds, and extinction transitions for all active emotions.

7. Mood Manager (`MoodManager`):
   - Manages the continuous three-dimensional circumplex mood state ($\vec{M} = \langle V, A, D \rangle$), integrating emotional history via damped moving averages.

8. Emotional Conflict Manager (`ConflictManager`):
   - Detects ambivalent and contradictory emotional pairs; calculates internal emotional tension metrics ($\Delta_{\text{tension}}$) without cancelling opposing emotions.

9. Emotion Regulation Module (`RegulationModule`):
   - Dispatches and monitors cognitive reappraisal, attentional shift, and pacing heuristics to dampen acute emotional surges while preserving audit traces.

10. Emotional Tendency Manager (`TendencyManager`):
    - Stores and applies Diva's unique baseline sensitivity multipliers and recovery profiles, providing authentic individual consistency.

11. Emotional Learning Interface (`LearningInterface`):
    - Validates and applies owner-directed parameter change requests from Learning Engine v2.5.0; manages internal parameter versioning.

12. Emotional Lifecycle Manager (`LifecycleManager`):
    - Authoritative transaction manager enforcing relational invariants, managing atomic commits, and logging all transitions in `emotion_lifecycle_events`.

PART 3 — INTER-ENGINE COMMUNICATION CONTRACTS
================================================================================

The Emotion Engine interfaces directly with ten adjacent engines via strongly typed, asynchronous contracts:

1. Perception Engine (`PerceptionContextProvider`):
   - Ingress: Ingests normalized sensory events, speech prosody cues, and environmental hazard/opportunity markers.

2. Memory Engine (`MemoryContextProvider`):
   - Ingress: Retrieves emotional history and past episodic affective associations.
   - Egress: Nominates high-significance emotional events ($S \ge 0.75$) for permanent episodic memory consolidation.

3. Thought Engine (`ThoughtContextProvider`):
   - Ingress: Ingests reflections, logical deductions, and counterfactual possibilities.
   - Egress: Dispatches affective state vectors ($E_{\text{resonance}}$) that weight thought priority and heuristic search.

4. Motivation Engine (`MotivationContextProvider`):
   - Ingress: Ingests goal progress milestones (satisfaction) and chronic desire blocks (frustration).
   - Egress: Dispatches emotional intensity vectors that amplify or dampen drive strength and effort willingness.

5. Learning Engine (`LearningContract`):
   - Ingress: Receives `ParameterChangeRequest` targeting `emotion.*` dimensions.
   - Egress: Returns `OwnerAcknowledgement` and `OwnerApplicationReceipt` with verified parameter versions.

6. Personality Engine (`PersonalityContextProvider`):
   - Ingress: Reads static constitutional baseline traits (introversion/extroversion, baseline optimism/caution) that parameterize emotional sensitivity.

7. Attention Control (`AttentionContract`):
   - Egress: Dispatches high-intensity emotional alerts ($I \ge 0.85$), requesting global attention allocation to critical emotional stimuli.

8. Energy Regulation (`EnergyContract`):
   - Ingress: Ingests current cognitive/physiological energy $E_{\text{cog}}$; low energy biases mood toward reduced arousal and subdued reactivity.

9. Social Cognition (`SocialCognitionProvider`):
   - Ingress: Ingests interpersonal trust models, social boundary norms, and perceived interlocutor rapport.

10. Cognitive Integration & Decision Engine (`CognitiveIntegrationContract`):
    - Egress: Transmits comprehensive `EmotionalStateSnapshot` (active emotions, intensities, mood vector, tension metric).
    - Invariant: Cognitive Integration uses emotional state to modulate speech tone and decision weighting; Emotion Engine never directly generates conversational text or executes external actions.

4. `EmotionTrigger`:

   - `trigger_type`: String (e.g., "UNEXPECTED_COMPLIMENT", "TASK_OBSTRUCTION").
   - `assessed_significance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `perceived_valence`: Float in [-1.0, 1.0].
   - `timestamp`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

5. `EmotionContext`:
   - `context_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `emotion_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `social_interlocutor_id`: Optional String.
   - `environmental_setting`: String.
   - `active_task_id`: Optional String.
   - `uncertainty_score`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `context_metadata`: Immutable Mapping[str, Any].

6. `EmotionTransition`:
   - `transition_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `emotion_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `previous_state`: String.
   - `new_state`: String.
   - `triggering_factor`: String.
   - `intensity_delta`: Float.
   - `transitioned_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

7. `MixedEmotionState`:
   - `mixed_state_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `coexisting_emotion_ids`: Immutable Tuple of UUIDs.
   - `primary_blend_name`: Optional String (e.g., "Bittersweetness", "Nostalgic Longing").
   - `coherence_score`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `observed_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

8. `EmotionalConflict`:
   - `conflict_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `emotion_a_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `emotion_b_id`: UUID (Foreign Key).
   - `conflict_category`: Enum (`VALENCE_OPPOSITION`, `MOTIVATIONAL_INTERFERENCE`, `SOCIAL_AMBIVALENCE`).
   - `tension_metric`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `is_active`: Boolean.
   - `detected_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

9. `MoodRecord`:
   - `mood_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `valence`: Float in [-1.0, 1.0].
   - `arousal`: Float in [-1.0, 1.0].
   - `dominance`: Float in [-1.0, 1.0].
   - `label_descriptor`: String (e.g., "Reflective Melancholy", "Buoyant Optimism").
   - `stability_metric`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `updated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

10. `MoodHistory`:
    - `history_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
    - `valence_snapshot`: Float.
    - `arousal_snapshot`: Float.
    - `dominance_snapshot`: Float.
