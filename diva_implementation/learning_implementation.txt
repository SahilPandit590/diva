A. SEVEN CORE MODULES AND THEIR EXPLICIT RESPONSIBILITIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The Learning Engine architecture preserves the seven original logical modules. Under no circumstance are these modules merged into a monolithic "learning agent." Each module maintains a strictly bounded functional responsibility:

A1. FORMALIZE THE BOUNDARY BETWEEN EXPERIENCE EVALUATION AND FEEDBACK SERVICE
Problem Addressed: Feedback reception and experience interpretation previously overlapped, risking unvalidated external signals directly affecting learning balances.
Architectural Specification:
The roles of Feedback Service and Experience Evaluation are strictly demarcated:

1. Feedback Service (`FeedbackService`):
   - Responsibility: Passive, protocol-level boundary ingress. Receives raw feedback events, environmental reports, and inter-engine signals.
   - Operations:
     * Validates message schema, structural integrity, and cryptographic/system origin signatures.
     * Enforces source authentication and validates that the emitting engine has authority to report on the referenced event.
     * Performs idempotency checks to intercept duplicate network or bus transmissions.
     * Normalizes raw payloads into standard internal `LearningSignal` envelopes.
   - Invariant: The Feedback Service NEVER evaluates whether an experience occurred, whether it was significant, or whether Diva learned something. It is an ingress validation and normalization gateway only.

2. Experience Evaluation (`ExperienceEvaluator`):
   - Responsibility: Active cognitive and context evaluation. Analyzes normalized signals to determine what actually occurred and what it means for Diva's cognitive baseline.
   - Operations:
     * Correlates the signal with active working context, episodic memories, and environmental ground truth.
     * Computes the objective outcome gap: $\Delta_{\text{outcome}} = \text{ObservedOutcome} - \text{ExpectedOutcome}$.
     * Evaluates multi-factor experience significance (magnitude, novelty, context, and potential consequence).
     * Discards trivial or ungrounded noise.
     * Formulates validated, structured `LearningContribution` instances.
   - Invariant: Experience Evaluation determines the cognitive weight of an individual event, but does NOT modify balances, detect multi-event trends, or authorize parameter changes.

A2. DEFINE LEARNING ASSESSMENT AS THE EXCLUSIVE PROPOSAL DECISION AUTHORITY
Problem Addressed: Pattern Recognition or Experience Evaluation previously attempted to propose parameter mutations directly.
Architectural Specification:
The decision-making authority for all learning proposals is centralized exclusively in `LearningAssessment`:
- Division of Epistemic Labor:
  * Experience Evaluation evaluates *individual events*.
  * Pattern Recognition detects *recurring trends across events*.
  * Learning Assessment decides *whether accumulated evidence warrants an actual change*.
- Mandatory Assessment Criteria:
  Before approving any learning proposal, Learning Assessment must formally evaluate:
  1. Evidence Sufficiency: Does the accumulated evidence exceed the dimension's minimum sample size ($N \ge N_{\text{min}}$)?
  2. Evidence Independence: Are the contributing experiences causally independent, or do they share underlying root causes?
  3. Pattern Recurrence: Has the behavioral tendency persisted across multiple temporal windows and context variations?
  4. Change Justification: Is the proposed modification mathematically warranted, or is it explainable by baseline noise?
  5. Temporal Freshness: Has sufficient evidence arrived within the active recency window, or is the evidence stale?
  6. Safety & Boundedness: Does the proposed adjustment comply with hard systemic safety constraints and parameter envelopes?
  7. Expected Utility: Is the change likely to measurably improve future task accuracy, emotional stability, or energy efficiency?
- Output Contract: Learning Assessment produces a structured `LearningAssessmentDecision` (Approved, Insufficient Evidence, Contradicted, or Unsafe), accompanied by full statistical justification.

A3. FORMALIZE DEVELOPMENT COORDINATION
Problem Addressed: Development Coordination must not become an alternative owner that directly mutates other engines' internal states.
Architectural Specification:
`DevelopmentCoordinator` acts solely as an owner-directed orchestration dispatcher.
The 8-Step Coordination Protocol:
1. Receive Approved Proposal: Ingests verified `LearningProposal` from Learning Assessment.
2. Resolve Owning Engine: Looks up the authoritative owner in `LearningDimensionRegistry`.
3. Construct Formal Change Request: Packages a typed, owner-specific payload (`ParameterChangeRequest`, `CapabilityDevelopmentRequest`, or `KnowledgeUpdateProposal`).
4. Dispatch to Owner: Transmits the change request to the owning engine's formal change ingress with a unique `dispatch_attempt_id`.
5. Receive Immediate Acknowledgement: Verifies whether the owner accepted or rejected the request.
6. Track Application Status: Monitors asynchronous execution and receives confirmed application receipts.
7. Request Post-Change Verification: Coordinates with Experience Evaluation to schedule an observation window.
8. Record Final Outcome: Inscribes the complete dispatch, application, and verification lifecycle in the immutable ledger.
Ownership Rule: Under no circumstance does Development Coordination write directly to memory tables, configuration files, neural weights, or runtime dictionaries of other engines.

A4. DEFINE LEARNING LIFECYCLE MANAGER AS THE COORDINATOR OF LEARNING RECORDS
Problem Addressed: Ambiguity regarding whether the lifecycle manager makes decisions or tracks state.
Architectural Specification:
The `LearningLifecycleManager` is the state-machine auditor of the Learning Engine. It owns the end-to-end lifecycle of contributions, assessments, and proposals:
- Managed Lifecycle Phases: `REGISTRATION` $\to$ `EVIDENCE_ACCUMULATION` $\to$ `ASSESSMENT` $\to$ `PROPOSAL_CREATION` $\to$ `DISPATCH` $\to$ `ACKNOWLEDGEMENT` $\to$ `APPLICATION_TRACKING` $\to$ `VERIFICATION` $\to$ `SUPERSEDED` / `REJECTED` / `CLOSED`.
- Invariant: The Lifecycle Manager cannot override the decisions of `LearningAssessment` or the rejection decisions of parameter-owning engines. It enforces state transition validity, manages timeout escalations, and ensures append-only audit persistence in SQLite.

B. LEARNING CATEGORIES AND OWNERSHIP BOUNDARIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

B1. PRESERVE THE THREE CANONICAL LEARNING CATEGORIES
Architectural Invariant:
The Learning Engine operates strictly across three canonical categories:
1. Parameter Learning: Calibration and continuous tuning of existing numerical, categorical, or probabilistic parameters owned by other engines.
2. Capability Learning: Expanding, refining, restructuring, or correcting functional cognitive skills, tool usage, and procedural workflows.
3. Knowledge Learning: Acquiring, correcting, corroborating, and structuring declarative, episodic, and semantic knowledge.
Boundary Rule: Relationship models are NOT a fourth learning category. Social relationships are modeled as specific domains within Knowledge Learning (declarative facts about entities) or Parameter Learning (social distance, trust calibration, warmth parameters).

B2. DEFINE PARAMETER LEARNING OWNERSHIP
Problem Addressed: Previous proposals lacked owner-directed constraints, risking out-of-bounds adjustments.
Architectural Specification:
Every parameter proposal emitted by the Learning Engine conforms to the strict `ParameterChangeRequest` contract:
- `owner_engine`: Enum (EMOTION, MOTIVATION, ATTENTION, ENERGY, PERSONALITY, PERCEPTION, THOUGHT, SOCIAL).
- `parameter_id`: Canonical, fully qualified identifier (e.g., `attention.distraction_threshold`, `emotion.joy_decay_rate`).
- `expected_current_value`: The current numerical or categorical state expected by Learning Engine.
- `expected_current_version`: Monotonically increasing version integer; prevents race conditions.
- `proposed_target_value`: Bounded target value.
- `proposed_adjustment`: Signed delta ($\Delta = \text{Target} - \text{Current}$).
- `valid_range`: Immutable tuple `(min_val, max_val)` enforced by the owning engine.
- `change_constraints`: Rate-limiting rules (e.g., max change $\le 0.05$ per update epoch).
- `supporting_evidence_ids`: Tuple of UUID strings pointing to immutable ledger entries.
- `expected_outcome`: Structured specification of anticipated behavioral impact.
- `rollback_strategy`: Explicit definition of prior stable version and reversion triggers.
Owning Engine Authority: The owning engine verifies `expected_current_version`. If the parameter was altered independently, the owner rejects the proposal with `VERSION_CONFLICT`. The Learning Engine never forces an update.

B3. DEFINE CAPABILITY LEARNING OWNERSHIP
Problem Addressed: Capability development can be confused with simple parameter tuning.
Architectural Specification:
Capability learning addresses procedural competence. Every `CapabilityDevelopmentRequest` distinguishes four development types:
1. `IMPROVING_EXISTING`: Optimizing latency, reducing token consumption, or refining heuristics of an established skill.
2. `EXTENDING_EXISTING`: Broadening the operational domain or input modalities of an existing tool/workflow.
3. `ACQUIRING_NEW`: Introducing a novel cognitive workflow, tool schema, or multi-step reasoning protocol.
4. `CORRECTING_FAILURE`: Patching an algorithmic edge-case, invalid premise rule, or broken integration.
Contract Fields:
- `capability_id`: Canonical capability identifier.
- `current_capability_level`: Integer or semantic tier (e.g., `NOVICE`, `COMPETENT`, `EXPERT`).
- `observed_limitation`: Detailed description of detected failure modes or bottlenecks.
- `evidence_references`: Trace IDs of failed executions and comparative benchmarks.
- `required_development_steps`: Structured recipe or curriculum for capability upgrade.
- `responsible_engine`: Owning engine (e.g., `ThoughtEngine` for reasoning rules, `PerceptionEngine` for feature extraction).
- `evaluation_criteria`: Unit test or benchmark suite required to validate competence.

B4. DEFINE KNOWLEDGE LEARNING OWNERSHIP
Problem Addressed: Overlap between Learning Engine and Memory Engine knowledge consolidation.
Architectural Specification:
- Division of Knowledge Authority:
  * The Learning Engine evaluates whether an experience, external correction, or reasoning reflection justifies a knowledge update.
  * The Learning Engine formulates a `KnowledgeUpdateProposal` containing proposed assertions, retractions, or confidence calibrations.
  * The Memory Engine remains the SOLE authoritative custodian of persistent memory graphs.
  * The Memory Engine inspects the proposal, verifies semantic coherence, updates belief graphs, manages link consolidation, and returns a verified `memory_record_id`.
  * The Learning Engine cannot write directly to SQLite or vector storage belonging to the Memory Engine.

B5. PROTECTED FOUNDATIONAL PERSONA VS. ADAPTABLE DEVELOPED PERSONALITY
Problem Addressed: Risk of unconstrained learning overwriting Diva's core identity, ethical safeguards, and defining characteristics.
Architectural Specification:
The architecture divides personality into two distinct conceptual and persistence layers:
1. `FoundationalPersona` (Protected Baseline):
   - Scope: Core character definitions, ethical boundaries, fundamental self-concept, constitutional guardrails, and non-negotiable personality invariants.
   - Ownership: Personality Engine (with strict system configuration locking).
   - Invariant: ORDINARY LEARNING PROPOSALS ARE PERMANENTLY PROHIBITED FROM MODIFYING FOUNDATIONAL PERSONA. Any proposal targeting a foundational attribute is rejected automatically by the registry with `PROTECTED_DIMENSION_VIOLATION`.
2. `DevelopedPersonality` (Adaptable Layer):
   - Scope: Contextual humor calibration, domain-specific conversational pacing, stylistic preferences, and acquired social nuances.
   - Ownership: Personality Engine.
   - Mutation Protocol: The Learning Engine may evaluate experiences and propose gradual, bounded adjustments to Developed Personality parameters, which the Personality Engine applies at its own discretion.

C. EXPERIENCE EVALUATION AND LEARNING SIGNALS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

C1. STANDARDIZE LEARNING CONTRIBUTIONS FROM EVERY SOURCE ENGINE
Problem Addressed: Adjacent engines submitted heterogeneous, incompatible learning observations.
Architectural Specification:
All learning contributions entering the Learning Engine must adhere to the standardized `LearningContribution` schema:

| Field | Type | Description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `contribution_id` | UUID | Globally unique identifier for this learning contribution. |
| `source_engine` | Enum | Emitting engine (`PERCEPTION`, `THOUGHT`, `EMOTION`, `MOTIVATION`, `MEMORY`, `INTEGRATION`). |
| `source_event_id` | String | External identifier from the emitting engine; used for idempotency checks. |
| `experience_id` | UUID | Canonical experience cluster identifier. |
| `contribution_type` | Enum | `EMPIRICAL_OUTCOME`, `INTERNAL_REFLECTION`, `EXTERNAL_FEEDBACK`, `PREDICTION_ERROR`. |
| `dimension_id` | String | Fully qualified target learning dimension defined in the registry. |
| `evidence_references`| Tuple[EvidenceRef] | Immutable tuple of underlying evidence records and sensor traces. |
| `observed_outcome` | Dict[str, Any] | Structured outcome data observed by the source engine. |
| `expected_outcome` | Dict[str, Any] | Structured outcome expected prior to the event. |
| `valence` | Enum | `POSITIVE_EVIDENCE`, `NEGATIVE_EVIDENCE`, `NEUTRAL_EVALUATIVE`. |
| `significance` | Float [0.0, 1.0] | Assessed cognitive importance and impact magnitude. |
| `confidence` | Float [0.0, 1.0] | Source engine's observation confidence. |
| `is_quarantined` | Boolean | Inherited from source; if True, contribution cannot trigger persistent parameter updates. |
| `created_at` | Timestamp | ISO-8601 UTC timestamp of original event occurrence. |
| `provenance` | Dict[str, Any] | Complete causal derivation history. |

C2. SEPARATE FEEDBACK, EXPERIENCE, CONTRIBUTION, AND PROPOSAL
Architectural Invariant:
The cognitive learning pipeline enforces a strict four-stage epistemic transformation:
```
[External Event / Sensor Data]
               │
               ▼
   1. Feedback Service          (Validates format, checks signatures, enforces idempotency)
               │
               ▼
   2. Experience Evaluation     (Evaluates context, outcome delta, significance, and quarantine)
               │
               ▼
   3. Learning Contribution     (Appends immutable evidence to the ledger, updates dimension balance)
               │
               ▼
   4. Learning Assessment       (Evaluates accumulated evidence sufficiency and authorizes Proposal)
```
- A raw feedback event is NOT an evaluated experience.
- An experience is NOT automatically evidence that a parameter is wrong.
- An accumulated evidence balance does NOT automatically generate a proposal.
- A learning proposal is NEVER a self-executed modification.

C3. MULTI-FACTOR EXPERIENCE SIGNIFICANCE FORMULATION
Problem Addressed: Trivial events were previously treated with the same weight as critical task failures.
Architectural Specification:
Experience significance $S_{\text{exp}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ is computed deterministically by `ExperienceEvaluator`:
$$S_{\text{exp}} = \text{clamp}_{[0.0, 1.0]}\left( w_O \cdot \Delta_{\text{outcome}} + w_R \cdot R_{\text{dim}} + w_N \cdot N_{\text{ctx}} + w_C \cdot C_{\text{impact}} \right) \times \text{Rel}_{\text{source}}$$
Where:
- $\Delta_{\text{outcome}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Magnitude of discrepancy between observed and expected outcomes.
- $R_{\text{dim}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Relevance of the event to the target learning dimension.
- $N_{\text{ctx}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Contextual novelty score (assessed against episodic memory baselines).
- $C_{\text{impact}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Potential systemic consequence of the outcome (safety, task completion, emotional toll).
- $\text{Rel}_{\text{source}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$: Certified reliability of the emitting engine or external user.
- Base Weights: $w_O = 0.35$, $w_R = 0.25$, $w_C = 0.25$, $w_N = 0.15$ (Sum = 1.00).

C4. DECOUPLING EVIDENCE VALENCE FROM PROPOSED CHANGE DIRECTION
Problem Addressed: Previous models assumed "negative evidence" meant a parameter must decrease, leading to illogical adjustments.
Architectural Specification:
The Learning Engine formally separates evidence interpretation from parameter direction:
1. `EvidenceValence`:
   - `POSITIVE_EVIDENCE`: The observed event confirms or strengthens the governing hypothesis.
   - `NEGATIVE_EVIDENCE`: The observed event refutes or weakens the governing hypothesis.
2. `ProposedChangeDirection`:
   - An independent decision made by `LearningAssessment`.
   - Example 1: In `attention.distraction_threshold`, negative evidence (Diva became distracted) requires INCREASING the threshold parameter to filter out noise.
   - Example 2: In `social.conversational_verbosity`, negative evidence (user requested brevity) requires DECREASING the verbosity parameter.
   - Invariant: The mathematical mapping between `EvidenceValence` and `ProposedChangeDirection` is explicitly codified in each dimension's `LearningAssessmentPolicy`.

D. EXPERIENCE POINT MANAGER AND EVIDENCE ACCUMULATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

D1. INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE BALANCES PER LEARNING DIMENSION
Problem Addressed: Evidence points were previously aggregated globally, allowing successes in social conversation to mask failures in mathematical reasoning.
Architectural Specification:
The `ExperiencePointManager` maintains strictly isolated, orthogonal evidence balances for every registered dimension:
- Every dimension $D_k$ tracks:
  * $E_{\text{pos}}(D_k)$: Cumulative positive evidence mass.
  * $E_{\text{neg}}(D_k)$: Cumulative negative evidence mass.
  * $N_{\text{obs}}(D_k)$: Total independent observation count.
  * $T_{\text{last}}(D_k)$: Timestamp of most recent contribution.
- Cross-Dimension Invariant: Evidence points NEVER cross dimension boundaries. A mathematical reasoning failure cannot cancel an emotional stability success.

D2. FORMAL LEARNINGDIMENSIONREGISTRY AND OWNERSHIP COVERAGE
Problem Addressed: Contributions could reference arbitrary, undefined dimension strings.
Architectural Specification:
Every learning dimension must be statically or dynamically declared in the authoritative `LearningDimensionRegistry`:

`LearningDimension` Schema:
- `dimension_id`: String (Canonical hierarchical identifier, e.g., `cognition.reasoning.deductive_rigor`).
- `category`: Enum (`PARAMETER_LEARNING`, `CAPABILITY_LEARNING`, `KNOWLEDGE_LEARNING`).
- `owning_engine`: Enum identifying the sole engine authorized to apply changes.
- `target_attribute`: Fully qualified parameter, capability, or knowledge domain name.
- `evidence_requirements`: Minimum sample size, required evidence types, and minimum confidence.
- `permitted_change_types`: Tuple of allowed strategies (`GRADUAL_ADJUSTMENT`, `BOUNDED_ADJUSTMENT`, `NO_CHANGE`).
- `valid_range`: Optional numerical or categorical bounds.
- `is_protected`: Boolean. If True, ordinary learning proposals are rejected; requires administrative override.
- `assessment_policy`: Instance of `LearningAssessmentPolicy` specifying decision thresholds.

Mandatory Baseline Coverage:
The registry must provide validated definitions for:
- `personality.*` (Owned by Personality Engine; foundational attributes marked `is_protected = True`).
- `emotion.*` (Owned by Emotion Engine; decay rates, reactivity baselines).
- `motivation.*` (Owned by Motivation Engine; goal priority weights, curiosity thresholds).
- `attention.*` (Owned by Attention Control; distraction thresholds, focal dwell times).
- `energy.*` (Owned by Energy Regulation; cognitive budget limits, expenditure rates).
- `memory.*` (Owned by Memory Engine; semantic verification thresholds, consolidation weights).
- `social.*` (Owned by Social Cognition; conversational pacing, empathy weights).
- `capability.*` (Owned by relevant functional subsystems; tool usage, reasoning heuristics).

D3. SEPARATION OF EVIDENCE DECAY FROM PARAMETER/STATE DECAY
Problem Addressed: Evidence decay was mistakenly used as an instruction to decay the actual parameter value in the owning engine.
Architectural Specification:
Two completely independent decay dynamics are maintained:
1. Evidence Decay (Internal to Learning Engine):
   - Older evidence contributions naturally lose epistemic weight over time to account for shifting environmental conditions.
   - Effective Evidence Calculation:
     $$E_{\text{eff}}(t) = \sum_{i} w_i \cdot \exp\left(-\lambda_{\text{evidence}} \cdot (t - t_i)\right)$$
   - Purpose: Prevents historical evidence from locking a dimension permanently.
2. Parameter / State Decay (Internal to Owning Engine):
   - Governed entirely by the owning engine's own internal dynamics (e.g., Emotion Engine decaying an emotional state over minutes).
   - Invariant: Evidence decay NEVER triggers parameter reversal. The Learning Engine only emits proposals when *new accumulated evidence* justifies a change.

D4. TRANSACTIONAL CONSISTENCY OF RUNNING EVIDENCE BALANCES
Problem Addressed: Power failure or process crash between appending a ledger entry and updating in-memory balances caused persistent drift.
Architectural Specification:
Every evidence contribution is processed inside an atomic SQLite transaction:
```sql
BEGIN IMMEDIATE;
-- 1. Check idempotency: ensure source_event_id has not been processed
-- 2. Insert row into experience_ledger (Immutable append-only record)
-- 3. Update running totals in evidence_balances table
-- 4. Inscribe sequence checkpoint
COMMIT;
```
If any constraint fails, the entire transaction rolls back.
Reconstructability Invariant:
In-memory and cached evidence balances are strictly derived views. Upon startup or audit check, the engine can completely reconstruct all dimensional balances by replaying the append-only `experience_ledger` from sequence 0.

E. PATTERN RECOGNITION AND STATISTICAL ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

E1. OPERATIONAL DEFINITION OF RECURRING PATTERNS AND CLASSIFICATIONS
Problem Addressed: Pattern Recognition previously treated repeated reports of a single incident as independent recurring experiences.
Architectural Specification:
A pattern is an abstraction discovered across multiple causally independent learning contributions.

Recurring Pattern Classifications:
1. `RECURRING_SUCCESS`: Consistent achievement of expected outcomes across diverse contextual environments ($N \ge 5$ independent successes; positive ratio $\ge 80\%$).
2. `RECURRING_FAILURE`: Persistent failure to achieve expected outcomes under similar task profiles ($N \ge 3$ independent failures; negative ratio $\ge 70\%$).
3. `BEHAVIORAL_TENDENCY`: A statistically significant drift or habitual response pattern that deviates from the normative baseline without necessarily producing an explicit task error.
4. `CONTEXT_DEPENDENT_PATTERN`: A pattern that exhibits high success in Context $A$ but consistent failure in Context $B$, indicating missing situational conditioning.
5. `CONTRADICTORY_PATTERN`: Bimodal outcome distribution where identical actions yield sharply diverging outcomes, signaling an unobserved latent variable.
6. `EMERGING_PATTERN`: Early evidence clusters that have achieved preliminary statistical significance ($p < 0.05$) but lack sufficient sample size for permanent parameter adjustment.

Pattern Invariant & Deduplication:
Pattern Recognition must cluster contributions on `independence_group_id`. If three contributions cite the same root sensory event or user interaction, they are treated as a SINGLE observation.

E2. BOUNDARY BETWEEN PATTERN DETECTION AND LEARNING ASSESSMENT
Problem Addressed: Pattern detection previously made prescriptive decisions about parameter updates.
Architectural Specification:
The boundary between discovery and authorization is strictly enforced:
- Pattern Recognition outputs an immutable `RecognizedPattern`:
  * `pattern_id`: UUID string.
  * `classification`: One of the 6 pattern enums above.
  * `dimension_id`: Fully qualified target dimension.
  * `contributing_ids`: Tuple of UUID strings pointing to supporting ledger entries.
  * `occurrence_count`: Number of verified independent events.
  * `temporal_span`: Duration between first and last observed occurrences.
  * `context_diversity`: Score in [0.0, 1.0] reflecting variance across situational contexts.
  * `statistical_significance`: Calculated $p$-value or test statistic.
- Learning Assessment consumes `RecognizedPattern` and decides:
  * Does this pattern warrant an actual modification to an engine parameter?
  * What is the safe, bounded adjustment magnitude?
  * What is the risk profile of modifying this parameter?
- Authority Rule: Pattern Recognition NEVER generates `LearningProposal` instances or calculates target parameter values.

E3. STATISTICAL ASSUMPTIONS AND CALIBRATION OF CUSUM
Problem Addressed: Cumulative Sum (CUSUM) change-point detection was previously cited without specifying reference values, standardized inputs, or false-alarm rates.
Architectural Specification:
The Learning Engine deploys two-sided, standardized CUSUM detectors for continuous parameter drift monitoring:

Statistical Formulation:
Let $x_t$ be the standardized outcome delta for observation $t$:
$$z_t = \frac{x_t - \mu_0}{\sigma_0}$$
Where $\mu_0$ is the historical baseline mean and $\sigma_0$ is the baseline standard deviation.
Two-sided cumulative sums are tracked independently:
$$S_t^+ = \max\left(0, S_{t-1}^+ + z_t - k\right)$$
$$S_t^- = \max\left(0, S_{t-1}^- - z_t - k\right)$$
Where:
- $k$: Reference value (slack parameter), set to $k = \frac{\delta}{2} = 0.50$, optimized to detect a 1.0 standard deviation shift ($\delta = 1.0$).
- $h$: Decision threshold, calibrated to achieve an Average Run Length under normal conditions ($\text{ARL}_0 \ge 500$ observations, corresponding to $h = 4.5$).

Detector Operational Invariants:
1. When $S_t^+ \ge h$, a positive change-point is flagged; detector signals upward drift.
2. When $S_t^- \ge h$, a negative change-point is flagged; detector signals downward drift.
3. Upon triggering, the detector resets $S_t = 0$ and logs a change-point event in the ledger.
4. Input Independence Mandate: Observations entering CUSUM must have $t_{i} - t_{i-1} \ge \tau_{\text{decorr}}$ to guarantee independence and prevent false alarms from autocorrelated sensory noise.

E4. MATHEMATICALLY RIGOROUS BINOMIAL TESTING AND SEQUENTIAL CONTROL
Problem Addressed: Binary turn outcomes (success vs. failure) were evaluated without explicit hypotheses, directionality, or multiple-testing corrections.
Architectural Specification:
When evaluating binary success/failure performance against a baseline success rate $p_0$:
- Hypotheses:
  * Null Hypothesis $H_0$: Current true success rate $p = p_0$.
  * Alternative Hypothesis $H_1$: True success rate $p \ne p_0$ (two-sided) or $p > p_0$ (one-sided, permitted ONLY when direction was pre-specified).
- Exact Binomial Test Calculation:
  For $k$ successes observed in $n$ independent trials:
  $$P(X \ge k) = \sum_{j=k}^{n} \binom{n}{j} p_0^j (1 - p_0)^{n-j}$$
- Multiple-Testing Correction:
  Because the Learning Engine evaluates dozens of dimensions simultaneously, the False Discovery Rate (FDR) is controlled via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure:
  $$p_{(i)} \le \frac{i}{M} \cdot Q$$
  Where $M$ is the number of monitored dimensions and $Q = 0.05$. An uncorrected $p < 0.05$ on a single dimension is insufficient to trigger an update if it fails the FDR threshold.
- Sequential Monitoring Stopping Policy:
  To prevent false positives from continuous peeking, early stopping requires crossing an O'Brien-Fleming boundary or achieving minimum sample size $n \ge 20$.

E5. PLATT CALIBRATION TRAINING, VALIDATION, AND LEAKAGE PREVENTION
Problem Addressed: Platt scaling was claimed without specifying training data separation, validation metrics, or fallback behavior.
Architectural Specification:
Platt scaling maps raw heuristic or neural decision scores $s \in \mathbb{R}$ onto true posterior probabilities $P(Y=1 \mid s) \in [0.0, 1.0]$:
$$P(Y=1 \mid s) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(A \cdot s + B)}$$
Protocol Requirements:
1. Calibration Dataset: Must comprise at least 100 independently validated historical experiences with ground-truth labels.
2. Data Leakage Prevention: The calibration set is partitioned using 5-fold cross-validation; parameters $A$ and $B$ are fitted on validation folds completely separate from the primary model training data.
3. Quality Metric: Calibration is certified if and only if Brier score improves by $\ge 15\%$ over raw scores and Expected Calibration Error (ECE) satisfies $\text{ECE} \le 0.05$.
4. Insufficient Data Fallback: If fewer than 100 labeled instances exist for a dimension, Platt scaling is DISABLED. Raw scores are bounded using conservative Laplace smoothing ($P = \frac{k+1}{n+2}$) rather than uncalibrated sigmoid transformations.

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

F. LEARNING PROPOSALS AND DEVELOPMENT COORDINATION

F1. EXPLICIT CURRENT AND TARGET PARAMETER VALUES WITH VERSION GUARDS
Problem Addressed: Proposals that vaguely requested "increase parameter" caused ambiguous state mutations and concurrency bugs.
Architectural Specification:
Every `ParameterChangeRequest` emitted by Development Coordination contains absolute state declarations:
- `parameter_id`: String (e.g., `attention.distraction_threshold`).
- `expected_current_value`: Float / String (e.g., `0.45`).
- `expected_current_version`: Integer (e.g., `14`).
- `proposed_target_value`: Float / String (e.g., `0.50`).
- `proposed_delta`: Float (e.g., `+0.05`).
- `version_guard_mandate`: The owning engine MUST evaluate:
  $$\text{assert}(\text{Engine}.\text{get\_version}(\text{parameter\_id}) == \text{expected\_current\_version})$$
  If the owner's internal version is $\ne 14$, the update is aborted with `VERSION_GUARD_FAILED`.

F2. GRADUAL VS. EXCEPTIONAL CHANGE POLICIES AND MAGNITUDE BOUNDS
Architectural Invariant:
All parameter modifications are classified into strict operational strategies:
1. `GRADUAL_ADJUSTMENT` (Default):
   - Maximum adjustment per update epoch: $|\Delta| \le 0.05$ (or $5\%$ of dynamic range).
   - Minimum interval between adjustments: $\Delta t \ge 3600\text{ seconds}$ (1 hour of active operation).
   - Purpose: Continuous smooth adaptation without behavioral instability.
2. `BOUNDED_ADJUSTMENT`:
   - Enforced by hard mathematical clamping against the registry's `valid_range`.
   - Even if evidence supports $\Delta = +0.20$, the proposal clamps target to $\min(\text{Target}, \text{Range}_{\text{max}})$.
3. `EXCEPTIONAL_CHANGE`:
   - Reserved for emergency safety patches or critical capability failure corrections.
   - Requires explicit multi-source corroboration (confidence $\ge 0.98$, $N \ge 10$) and cognitive integration executive authorization.
   - INVARIANT: Exceptional changes can NEVER bypass Foundational Persona protections.
4. `NO_CHANGE`:
   - Emitted when evidence confirms the current parameter is already operating at optimal utility.

F3. UNIQUE DISPATCH ATTEMPT IDENTIFIERS AND RETRY TRACKING
Problem Addressed: Retried message deliveries were mistaken for duplicate proposals.
Architectural Specification:
The Learning Engine decouples proposal identity from dispatch transmission identity:
- `proposal_id`: UUID string identifying the cognitive decision (Immutable).
- `dispatch_attempt_id`: UUID string generated fresh for EVERY individual network/bus transmission attempt.
- Dispatch Audit Record (`DispatchAttempt`):
  * `dispatch_attempt_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
  * `proposal_id`: UUID (Foreign Key to `LearningProposal`).
  * `target_engine`: Enum.
  * `attempt_number`: Integer ($1, 2, 3$).
  * `dispatched_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
  * `delivery_status`: Enum (`IN_FLIGHT`, `ACKNOWLEDGED`, `TIMED_OUT`, `FAILED_TRANSPORT`).
  * `acknowledgement_id`: Optional UUID string linking to owner response.

F4. THIRTEEN-STAGE PROPOSAL LIFECYCLE AND DISTINCT ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS
Problem Addressed: Receiving a message was previously conflated with accepting, applying, and verifying a change.
Architectural Specification:
The `LearningLifecycleManager` enforces 13 mutually exclusive lifecycle states:
```
1. CREATED                -> Proposal generated by Learning Assessment
2. READY_FOR_DISPATCH     -> Pre-dispatch validation checks passed
3. DISPATCHED             -> In-flight on the inter-engine bus
4. RECEIVED               -> Owning engine acknowledges receipt of payload
5. ACCEPTED               -> Owning engine confirms proposal is valid and will apply
6. REJECTED               -> Owning engine rejects proposal (version conflict, constraint violation)
7. APPLICATION_PENDING    -> Owning engine has queued application for next safe cycle
8. APPLIED                -> Owning engine has committed change to its internal state
9. VERIFICATION_PENDING   -> Post-change observation window initiated
10. VERIFIED              -> Post-change metrics confirm expected improvement
11. FAILED                -> Post-change metrics detect regression or failure
12. SUPERSEDED            -> Newer proposal rendered this proposal obsolete
13. CANCELLED             -> Manually or systematically cancelled prior to application
```

F5. STRUCTURED OWNER APPLICATION REPORTING CONTRACTS
Problem Addressed: Learning Engine previously assumed changes were applied without verifiable receipts.
Architectural Specification:
When an owning engine applies a change, it transmits a structured `OwnerApplicationReceipt`:
- `receipt_id`: UUID string.
- `proposal_id`: UUID string matching the proposal.
- `dispatch_attempt_id`: UUID string matching the dispatch attempt.
- `owner_engine`: Enum identifying the reporting engine.
- `application_status`: Enum (`SUCCESSFULLY_APPLIED`, `PARTIALLY_APPLIED`, `REJECTED_AT_APPLICATION`).
- `resulting_parameter_value`: The actual new value active in the owner engine.
- `resulting_version`: The new monotonically incremented version integer.
- `applied_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
- `owner_execution_hash`: Cryptographic or state hash verifying internal consistency.

F6. POST-CHANGE VERIFICATION PLANNING AND OBSERVATION WINDOWS
Problem Addressed: Applying a change was treated as the end of the learning process, ignoring post-change regressions.
Architectural Specification:
Every approved proposal includes an mandatory `VerificationPlan`:
- `observation_window_epochs`: Number of operational cycles or elapsed time (e.g., 50 task turns).
- `target_metric`: Measurable performance indicator (e.g., task latency, user correction frequency).
- `success_threshold`: Minimum statistical improvement required to mark `VERIFIED`.
- `regression_threshold`: Performance drop that immediately triggers a regression alert.
Operational Rule: If performance degrades below `regression_threshold` during the observation window, the Lifecycle Manager transitions the proposal to `FAILED` and triggers the Rollback Protocol.

F7. OWNER-DIRECTED ROLLBACK AND REGRESSION HANDLING PROTOCOLS
Problem Addressed: The Learning Engine previously attempted to restore previous parameters directly during a rollback.
Architectural Specification:
Rollback authority belongs exclusively to the parameter's owning engine:
1. Learning Assessment detects regression during post-change verification.
2. Development Coordination constructs a formal `RollbackRequest`:
   * `rollback_request_id`: UUID string.
   * `original_proposal_id`: UUID string of the failed change.
   * `target_engine`: Owning engine.
   * `parameter_id`: Parameter to restore.
   * `known_good_version`: The historical version recorded in the original proposal.
   * `known_good_value`: The historical value recorded in the original proposal.
   * `regression_evidence_ids`: Ledger IDs proving the post-change performance drop.
3. Owning engine verifies `known_good_version`, restores the previous stable parameter value, increments its internal version, and transmits a confirmed `RollbackReceipt`.
4. Learning Engine records the rollback in its immutable ledger and temporarily locks the dimension against re-proposing the failed value.

G1. FORMAL THOUGHT ENGINE REFLECTION AND ERROR SIGNAL INTEGRATION

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
H. IMMUTABLE LEDGER AND DATABASE CORRECTNESS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

H1. TRUE DEFENSIVE-COPY REGISTRY IMMUTABILITY AND RECURSIVE FREEZING
Problem Addressed: Wrapping a mutable dictionary in `MappingProxyType` leaves the underlying dictionary vulnerable to external mutation through retained references.
Architectural Specification:
The `LearningDimensionRegistry` enforces true deep immutability:
1. Defensive Deep Copy: When the registry is constructed or initialized, all backing data structures are deep-copied.
2. Recursive Freezing:
   - All nested lists are converted to immutable `tuple`.
   - All nested sets are converted to `frozenset`.
   - All nested dictionaries are wrapped in nested `MappingProxyType` instances.
3. Reference Disposal: The original mutable initialization dictionaries are destroyed and dereferenced; no external reference to any mutable container survives.
4. Mutation Lockdown: Attempting to insert, overwrite, or mutate a registered dimension raises an uncatchable `RegistryFrozenError`.

H2. COMPLETE DATABASE-LEVEL APPEND-ONLY LEDGER TRIGGERS
Problem Addressed: Application-level conventions failed to protect the ledger against raw SQL updates or accidental cascading deletions.
Architectural Specification:
The SQLite database enforces physical, database-level append-only invariants via triggers on `experience_ledger`:

```sql
-- Enforce absolute prohibition of UPDATE on the ledger
CREATE TRIGGER IF NOT EXISTS trg_prevent_ledger_update
BEFORE UPDATE ON experience_ledger
BEGIN
    SELECT RAISE(ABORT, 'CRITICAL_SECURITY_VIOLATION: Updates to experience_ledger are strictly prohibited.');
END;

-- Enforce absolute prohibition of DELETE on the ledger
CREATE TRIGGER IF NOT EXISTS trg_prevent_ledger_delete
BEFORE DELETE ON experience_ledger
BEGIN
    SELECT RAISE(ABORT, 'CRITICAL_SECURITY_VIOLATION: Deletions from experience_ledger are strictly prohibited.');
END;

-- Enforce absolute prohibition of replacement-style writes
CREATE TRIGGER IF NOT EXISTS trg_prevent_ledger_replace
BEFORE INSERT ON experience_ledger
WHEN EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM experience_ledger WHERE contribution_id = NEW.contribution_id)
BEGIN
    SELECT RAISE(ABORT, 'IDEMPOTENCY_VIOLATION: Contribution ID already exists in experience_ledger.');
END;
```

H3. FULL CONTEXTUAL LEDGER PARTITIONING (INCLUDING SITUATIONAL MODE)
Problem Addressed: Situational mode was omitted from ledger indexes, causing evidence from play, emergency, or formal modes to be conflated.
Architectural Specification:
Every record in `experience_ledger` is indexed across all six canonical contextual axes:
1. `dimension_id`: The target learning dimension.
2. `situation_context`: Domain or topic identifier (e.g., `technical_coding`, `creative_writing`).
3. `situational_mode`: Enum (`CONVERSATIONAL_CASUAL`, `FOCUSED_WORK`, `CRITICAL_INCIDENT`, `EXPLORATORY_PLAY`).
4. `evidence_category`: `EMPIRICAL`, `FEEDBACK`, `REFLECTION`.
5. `owner_engine_id`: Authoritative engine responsible for the target attribute.
6. `observation_period`: Discrete temporal cohort (e.g., 2026-W46).
Compound Index Definition:
```sql
CREATE INDEX idx_ledger_contextual_partition 
ON experience_ledger (dimension_id, situational_mode, situation_context, observation_period);
```
Rule: Pattern recognition and learning assessment cannot aggregate evidence across divergent `situational_mode` values without an explicit `GeneralizationAssessment`.

H4. STRONGLY TYPED, STRUCTURED OWNER ACKNOWLEDGEMENT RECORDS
Problem Addressed: Using untyped `Any` payloads undermined schema validation and allowed corrupted receipts.
Architectural Specification:
All responses from owner engines conform to the strongly typed `OwnerAcknowledgement` schema:

```python
# Conceptual Schema Representation
class OwnerAcknowledgement:
    acknowledgement_id: UUID
    proposal_id: UUID
    dispatch_attempt_id: UUID
    target_engine: EngineType
    acknowledgement_status: AckStatus  # RECEIVED, ACCEPTED, REJECTED, INVALID_SCHEMA
    owner_current_version: int

    rejection_reason: Optional[str]
    timestamp: str  # ISO-8601 UTC
    signature: str  # Engine cryptographic/system signature
```
Under no circumstance are unstructured dictionaries or primitive strings accepted as acknowledgements.

H5. DETERMINISTIC LEDGER BALANCE RECONCILIATION AND REBUILD PROTOCOLS
Problem Addressed: Cached running balances could drift from the immutable ledger after sudden power loss.
Architectural Specification:
The append-only `experience_ledger` is the SOLE authoritative source of truth.
Reconciliation Protocol:
1. Periodic Integrity Audit: Runs every 1000 contributions or upon engine startup.
2. Rebuild Algorithm:
   - For every dimension $D_k$, recalculate:
     $$\hat{E}_{\text{pos}}(D_k) = \sum \{ \text{significance}_i \mid \text{valence}_i = \text{POSITIVE\_EVIDENCE} \}$$
     $$\hat{E}_{\text{neg}}(D_k) = \sum \{ \text{significance}_i \mid \text{valence}_i = \text{NEGATIVE\_EVIDENCE} \}$$
     $$\hat{N}_{\text{obs}}(D_k) = \text{Count}(\text{distinct } \text{independence\_group\_id})$$
3. Drift Detection: Compare $\hat{E}$ against cached `evidence_balances` table.
4. Auto-Correction: If a discrepancy exists, the cached table is updated to match $\hat{E}$. The immutable ledger is NEVER modified to match the cache.

H6. IDEMPOTENT CONTRIBUTION INGESTION AND DE-DUPLICATION
Problem Addressed: Duplicate delivery of sensory events or reflections resulted in double-counted evidence.
Architectural Specification:
Ingestion idempotency is guaranteed via a two-tier filter:
1. Tier 1 (Memory Filter): In-memory Bloom filter and LRU cache of recently processed `source_event_id` keys ($10,000$ key capacity).
2. Tier 2 (Database Constraint): Unique constraint on `(source_engine, source_event_id)` in the SQLite ledger table.
Protocol:
- If a contribution arrives with an existing `(source_engine, source_event_id)`:
  * Ingestion is immediately halted.
  * No new row is inserted into `experience_ledger`.
  * No balance is incremented.
  * The original `contribution_id` is returned with status `DUPLICATE_IGNORED`.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I. EVIDENCE INDEPENDENCE, DECAY, AND CONTEXTUAL GENERALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

I1. CORRELATED EVIDENCE DISCOUNTING AND LINEAGE DEDUPLICATION
Problem Addressed: Correlated sensory inputs (e.g., three cameras reporting the same gesture, or five logs of the same server crash) artificially inflated evidence balances.
Architectural Specification:
The `ExperienceEvaluator` assigns an `independence_group_id` to every incoming contribution:
- Definition: An `independence_group_id` identifies the underlying causal phenomenon generating the observation.
- Discounting Formula:
  When calculating total evidence for dimension $D_k$:
  $$E_{\text{effective}} = \sum_{g \in \text{Groups}} \max_{i \in g} (\text{Significance}_i \times \text{Confidence}_i)$$
  Within any single independence group, only the *strongest single observation* contributes to the balance. Secondary reports provide verification of occurrence, not additive evidence volume.

I2. MULTI-CONTEXT GENERALIZATION ASSESSMENT PROTOCOL
Problem Addressed: Diva learning that an aggressive pacing strategy worked in a competitive game automatically applying it to a supportive conversation.
Architectural Specification:
Before any parameter modification derived in Context $A$ is applied globally or to Context $B$, Learning Assessment must execute a `GeneralizationAssessment`:

Assessment Factors:
1. Source Context: The situational context where the pattern was discovered.
2. Target Context: The proposed operational context for the parameter.
3. Context Similarity Score ($S_{\text{ctx}} \in [0.0, 1.0]$): Semantic and relational distance between task requirements, user expectations, and emotional stakes.
4. Target Context Evidence: Has the proposed change ever been observed in the target context?
   - If positive evidence exists in target context: Generalization Approved.
   - If zero evidence exists and $S_{\text{ctx}} < 0.75$: Generalization REJECTED. The parameter adjustment is converted to a *context-conditioned override* scoped exclusively to Source Context.
   - If contradictory evidence exists in target context: Generalization STRICTLY FORBIDDEN.

I3. STATISTICAL RELIABILITY, CALIBRATION, AND SAFETY SUMMARY
The statistical methods governing the Learning Engine are unified under strict operational assumptions:

| Statistical Method | Input Requirements | Mathematical Assumption | Decision Threshold | Fallback / Safeguard |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Two-Sided CUSUM** | Standardized continuous $\Delta_{\text{outcome}}$ | Independent observations, Gaussian baseline | $S_t \ge 4.5$ ($\text{ARL}_0 \ge 500$) | Decorrelation time filter $\tau_{\text{decorr}}$ |
| **Exact Binomial** | Binary turn outcomes (Success/Failure) | Independent Bernoulli trials, fixed $p_0$ | $p < 0.05$ with FDR Benjamini-Hochberg | Minimum sample $n \ge 20$; O'Brien-Fleming |
| **Platt Scaling** | Decision heuristic scores $s \in \mathbb{R}$ | Monotonic sigmoid relationship | Brier improvement $\ge 15\%$, ECE $\le 0.05$| Laplace smoothing ($P = \frac{k+1}{n+2}$) |
| **Evidence Decay** | Historical contribution timestamps | Exponential loss of relevance | Half-life $\tau_{\text{decay}}$ per dimension | State in owner does NOT decay |
| **Significance** | Multi-factor outcome and context | Multi-attribute linear utility | $S_{\text{exp}} \ge 0.15$ threshold | Trivial noise discarded prior to ledger |
