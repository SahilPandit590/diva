EXECUTIVE ARCHITECTURAL SUMMARY & GOVERNING PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

The Social Cognition Engine governs Diva's internal representation of the social
world: how she recognizes individuals, builds structured models of their attributes
and behaviors, maintains evolving relationships, interprets social interactions,
estimates others' intentions and perspectives, understands group dynamics, and
develops contextual trust assessments — all grounded in accumulated evidence with
explicit uncertainty and provenance.

This engine does NOT simply recognize names or remember conversations. It maintains
structured, evidence-based social models that evolve continuously through experience.

Persistent historical storage is owned by the Memory Engine. Social Cognition owns
the current working interpretive models — structured representations that go beyond
raw data to embody Diva's social understanding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FIVE SUPREME NON-NEGOTIABLE AXIOMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. KNOWING SOMEONE IS NOT THE SAME AS UNDERSTANDING SOMEONE
   Identity, individual models, and relationships are three separate representations.
   Recognizing that "person_A42" exists (identity) is fundamentally different from
   understanding their interests, communication style, and behavioral patterns
   (individual model), which is fundamentally different from tracking how Diva's
   relationship with that person has developed over time (relationship record).
   These three layers must remain structurally independent.

2. INFERENCE IS NOT FACT
   Diva can estimate someone's intentions, predict their possible reactions, and
   model their beliefs, but MUST preserve the categorical distinction between:
   - What she directly observed (OBSERVED).
   - What was explicitly stated to her (REPORTED).
   - What she inferred from patterns (INFERRED).
   - What remains uncertain or speculative (HYPOTHESIZED).
   An inference must NEVER silently graduate to established fact without explicit
   evidence reclassification with supporting provenance.

3. RELATIONSHIPS ARE MULTIDIMENSIONAL
   Familiarity, trust, affinity, cooperation, communication quality, reciprocity,
   conflict, and closeness are eight independent dimensions with separate update
   rules, separate evidence bases, and separate confidence values. They must NEVER
   be collapsed into a single "relationship score." Someone can be highly familiar
   but untrusted. Someone can be trusted professionally but emotionally distant.

4. SOCIAL UNDERSTANDING MUST EVOLVE
   Diva must be able to revise her understanding of anyone when new experiences
   contradict previous assumptions. Social models are living, revisable structures
   with versioned history, not permanent personality stamps applied after a single
   interaction.

5. SOCIAL COGNITION DOES NOT OWN EMOTION, MOTIVATION, OR DECISION
   Social Cognition provides structured social interpretations, context, and
   assessments. The Emotion Engine evaluates emotional significance. The Motivation
   Engine handles social drives and desires. Cognitive Integration makes decisions.
   Social Cognition NEVER directly generates Diva's emotional states, creates
   motivational drives, executes external actions, or makes final behavioral decisions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ADDITIONAL ARCHITECTURAL SAFEGUARDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- IDENTITY IS NOT ACCOUNT OWNERSHIP: A different conversational speaker does not
  automatically become the account owner. Account identity, conversational identity,
  and entity identity are three distinct concepts.

- REPORTED INFORMATION IS NOT OBSERVED INFORMATION: Every person model preserves
  where information came from. If Diva's primary user says "My friend likes chess,"
  that is reported information about an indirectly known individual, not a direct
  observation by Diva.

- SOCIAL MODELS ARE NOT HISTORICAL MEMORY: Social Cognition owns current working
  models. Memory Engine owns persistent experiences and long-term retention. These
  are complementary, not competing systems.

- UNDERSTANDING IS NOT DECIDING: Social Cognition provides assessments. Cognitive
  Integration makes decisions. Social Cognition cannot decide whether Diva should
  forgive someone, confront them, or continue making plans.

- SOCIAL MODELS AS SENSITIVE INFORMATION: The engine must maintain provenance,
  access awareness, retention policies, and a clear distinction between information
  explicitly shared by someone and inferences Diva has made about them. This prevents
  the system from silently turning uncertain social guesses into permanent profiles.

1. INDIVIDUAL RECOGNITION & IDENTITY MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Diva must recognize that different interactions can involve the same individual,
and must maintain structured identity records that persist across interactions.

1.1 Three-Layer Identity Model:
    The engine distinguishes three fundamentally different identity concepts:

    a) Account Identity: Which user account or communication channel is being used.
       This is a technical identifier (e.g., Discord user ID, email address, API key).
       Assigned by the Perception Engine as part of message metadata.

    b) Conversational Identity: Who does the current interaction appear to come from?
       This may differ from account identity (someone else using the device, shared
       accounts, anonymous sessions). Estimated by Social Cognition using behavioral
       and contextual signals.

    c) Entity Identity: Which person is being discussed or referred to in conversation?
       A person mentioned by name in conversation is an entity, not necessarily the
       speaker. Social Cognition maintains entity records for discussed individuals.

    These three MUST NOT be conflated. Someone mentioned in conversation (entity) is
    not the same as the person speaking (conversational identity), who may not be the
    account holder (account identity).

1.2 Identity Assignment & Confidence:
    Every recognized individual receives a canonical `individual_id` (UUID).
    - New individual detection triggers immediate registration with minimal attributes.
    - Identity confidence $C_{id} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ tracks how certain the identification is.
    - $C_{id} \ge 0.90$: HIGH confidence — consistent behavioral and explicit evidence.
    - $0.60 \le C_{id} < 0.90$: MODERATE — some supporting signals, possible ambiguity.
    - $C_{id} < 0.60$: LOW — tentative identification, insufficient evidence.
    - $C_{id} = 0.0$: UNKNOWN — completely unidentified entity.

1.3 Identity Matching & Merge Prevention:
    When a new interaction arrives, the Identity Manager must determine whether the
    speaker matches an existing individual profile:

    Matching Algorithm:
    1. Extract explicit identifiers (account IDs, stated names, platform handles).
    2. Compare against existing identifier records with exact string matching.
    3. If exact match found with $C_{id} \ge 0.70$: Link to existing profile.
    4. If no exact match: Compute behavioral similarity using LLM-assisted analysis
       of communication patterns, vocabulary, topic preferences, and interaction style.
    5. Apply merge threshold: Two profiles may be merged ONLY when:
       - At least one explicit identifier match exists, AND
       - Behavioral similarity score $\ge 0.75$, AND
       - No contradictory evidence exists (conflicting locations, simultaneous
         interactions, inconsistent personal details).
    6. If merge evidence is suggestive but not conclusive ($0.50 \le S_{merge} < 0.75$):
       Create a `PossibleIdentityLink` record without merging. Flag for future
       evidence accumulation.

    Anti-Merge Safeguard:
    - Two individuals with the same display name are NEVER automatically merged.
    - Name collision creates a disambiguation record, not a merge event.
    - Only convergent multi-signal evidence triggers merge consideration.

    Anti-Duplication Safeguard:
    - Before creating a new profile, the Identity Manager checks existing profiles
      for identifier overlap and behavioral similarity.
    - If overlap score $\ge 0.85$ with an existing profile: Block creation, link to
      existing profile instead.

1.4 Identity Corrections:
    Identity assignments can be corrected through explicit evidence:
    - Direct statement: "That was actually my friend using my account."
    - Contradictory evidence accumulation.
    - User correction: "No, that's a different person."

    Correction Protocol:
    1. Record the correction as an `IdentityEvidence` with source `EXPLICIT_CORRECTION`.
    2. If a merged profile needs splitting: Create new profile, migrate attributed
       interactions, preserve audit trail.
    3. If separate profiles need merging: Execute merge with provenance preservation.
    4. Update all downstream references (relationship records, interaction attributions).
    5. Record correction in `social_model_history` for audit.

1.5 Identity Types:
    - `PERSISTENT`: Fully identified individual with stable canonical ID.
    - `TEMPORARY`: Interaction participant whose identity is session-scoped (e.g.,
      anonymous chat participant). May graduate to PERSISTENT with evidence.
    - `INDIRECT`: Person known only through others' descriptions (e.g., "my friend Rahul").
      No direct interaction exists. All attributes are REPORTED, not OBSERVED.
    - `COLLECTIVE`: An organization, community, or group entity (not an individual person).

1.6 Entity Type Discrimination:
    The engine must distinguish between:
    - Individual persons.
    - Organizations and companies.
    - Groups and communities.
    - Fictional characters or hypothetical persons.
    Each entity type has different modeling rules. Organizations do not have "emotions"
    or "intentions" in the same sense as individuals.

2. INDIVIDUAL MODELING & EVIDENCE-BASED ATTRIBUTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Recognizing someone is fundamentally different from understanding them. The engine
must gradually build an internal model of each individual through accumulated,
attributed evidence.

2.1 Attribute Categories:
    Individual models maintain structured attributes across six domains:

    a) Demographic & Identifying:
       - Known names and aliases.
       - Communication channels and identifiers.
       - Stated location, language, timezone (when explicitly shared).

    b) Interests & Preferences:
       - Topics of expressed interest.
       - Communication style preferences (verbose, brief, formal, casual).
       - Interaction timing preferences.
       - Content preferences (technical, creative, philosophical, casual).

    c) Behavioral Patterns:
       - Communication frequency and consistency.
       - Response time patterns.
       - Topic engagement patterns.
       - Emotional expression patterns.
       - Cooperation and reciprocity patterns.

    d) Cognitive & Capability:
       - Demonstrated knowledge domains.
       - Problem-solving approaches observed.
       - Expertise areas with supporting evidence.

    e) Relational:
       - Known relationships to other individuals.
       - Social roles (mentor, collaborator, friend).
       - Group memberships.

    f) Contextual & Temporal:
       - Current known circumstances (e.g., "preparing for an exam").
       - Stated goals and plans.
       - Life events mentioned.

2.2 Four-Level Evidence Attribution:
    Every attribute attached to an individual model carries a mandatory evidence
    classification:

    Level 1 — OBSERVED:
    Information derived from Diva's direct observation of the individual's behavior.
    Example: "This person consistently discusses programming topics" (observed across
    8 interactions). Confidence floor: 0.70 (strong behavioral evidence).

    Level 2 — REPORTED:
    Information explicitly stated by someone (the individual themselves, or a third
    party). Source attribution is mandatory.
    Example: "My friend Rahul is good at mathematics" — REPORTED by primary_user.
    Confidence floor: 0.50 (dependent on reporter's reliability).

    Level 3 — INFERRED:
    Information deduced by Diva from patterns, context, or reasoning.
    Example: "This person appears to have a sustained interest in robotics"
    (inferred from repeated discussion patterns).
    Confidence floor: 0.30 (hypothesis requiring further evidence).

    Level 4 — HYPOTHESIZED:
    Speculative information with minimal evidence support.
    Example: "This person might be a student" (single contextual clue).
    Confidence floor: 0.10 (tentative, may be discarded).

    Promotion Rules:
    - HYPOTHESIZED -> INFERRED: Requires $\ge 3$ supporting evidence instances
      with no contradictory evidence.
    - INFERRED -> OBSERVED: Requires $\ge 5$ direct behavioral observations
      spanning $\ge 3$ distinct interactions.
    - REPORTED information from a trusted source ($T_{reporter} \ge 0.80$) may be
      treated as equivalent to INFERRED but NEVER automatically to OBSERVED.
    - REPORTED -> OBSERVED: Only when Diva directly observes the same attribute.

    Demotion Rules:
    - Contradictory evidence triggers confidence reduction (not immediate deletion).
    - $\ge 3$ contradictory instances within recent interactions triggers
      reclassification to HYPOTHESIZED with updated confidence.
    - Stale information (no supporting evidence for $T_{stale}$ interactions)
      receives confidence decay: $C_{new} = C_{old} \cdot 0.95^{n_{stale}}$
      where $n_{stale}$ is the number of interactions since last supporting evidence.

2.3 Contradictory Information Handling:
    When new evidence contradicts existing attributes:
    1. Record both the existing and contradictory evidence.
    2. Do NOT immediately delete the existing attribute.
    3. Reduce confidence of the existing attribute by $\Delta C = -0.15$ per
       contradictory instance.
    4. Create a `ContradictionRecord` linking both evidence items.
    5. If confidence drops below $0.20$: Mark attribute as `DISPUTED`.
    6. Disputed attributes are excluded from Social Context Packages but preserved
       for historical reconstruction.

2.4 Temporal Relevance & Attribute Staleness:
    Attributes have temporal relevance that decays without reinforcement:
    - `current_relevance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0], initialized at 1.0.
    - Decay: $R(t) = R_0 \cdot \exp(-\lambda_R \cdot \Delta t_{interactions})$
      where $\lambda_R = 0.02$ per interaction-without-reinforcement.
    - Attributes with $R < 0.30$ are marked `STALE` but not deleted.
    - Stale attributes are excluded from active Social Context Packages but
      available for historical reconstruction.

2.5 Indirectly Known Individuals:
    Diva can create preliminary models of people she has never interacted with
    directly, based on information reported during conversations.
    - All attributes are classified as REPORTED with explicit source attribution.
    - The reporter's individual_id is recorded as the evidence source.
    - If the indirectly known person later interacts directly, the model transitions
      from INDIRECT to PERSISTENT, and new direct observations can supplement or
      contradict the reported information.

3. INTENTION & GOAL INTERPRETATION (THEORY OF MIND — BEHAVIORAL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine must produce structured hypotheses about other individuals' possible
intentions, goals, and motivations — WITHOUT treating those hypotheses as facts.

3.1 Intention Hypothesis Generation:
    For any socially significant interaction, the Intention Inference Module generates
    a ranked set of `IntentionHypothesis` records:

    Each hypothesis specifies:
    - `hypothesis_id`: UUID.
    - `subject_id`: UUID of the individual whose intentions are being estimated.
    - `interaction_id`: UUID of the triggering interaction.
    - `intention_type`: Enum (`INFORMATION_SEEKING`, `COOPERATION_OFFERING`,
      `ASSISTANCE_REQUESTING`, `PERSUASION_ATTEMPTING`, `COMPETITION_ENGAGING`,
      `SOCIAL_BONDING`, `CONFLICT_INITIATING`, `DECEPTION_POSSIBLE`,
      `BOUNDARY_TESTING`, `NEUTRAL_EXCHANGE`, `UNKNOWN`).
    - `description`: Natural language description of the hypothesized intention.
    - `confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `supporting_evidence`: Tuple of evidence record IDs.
    - `contradicting_evidence`: Tuple of evidence record IDs.
    - `status`: Enum (`ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `REFUTED`, `CONFIRMED`).

    Generation Rules:
    - Always generate $\ge 2$ hypotheses for any ambiguous interaction.
    - The sum of confidences across hypotheses for a single interaction MAY exceed
      1.0 (hypotheses are not mutually exclusive in all cases).
    - DECEPTION_POSSIBLE is NEVER generated as the sole or highest-confidence
      hypothesis without strong contradictory evidence between statements and actions.
    - Hypothesis confidence MUST decay with time: $C(t) = C_0 \cdot 0.90^{n_{interactions}}$
      unless reinforced by new supporting evidence.

3.2 Indirect Communication Interpretation:
    The module must handle indirect or implicit communication:
    - Requests disguised as statements ("It would be nice if someone helped with this").
    - Implications and subtext ("I noticed you haven't responded to my earlier message").
    - Rhetorical questions, sarcasm, and irony (flagged with high uncertainty).
    - Cultural or contextual communication norms that affect interpretation.

    LLM Integration Point: Indirect communication interpretation is explicitly
    delegated to shared LLM infrastructure. The LLM proposes interpretations;
    the Intention Inference Module validates, scores, and persists them.

3.3 Longitudinal Intention Tracking:
    When an individual's behavior across multiple interactions suggests a sustained
    goal or pattern:
    - Create an `IndividualGoalEstimate` linking multiple interaction-level hypotheses.
    - Track how the estimated goal progresses or changes across interactions.
    - Distinguish short-term conversational goals from long-term relational goals.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PERSPECTIVE-TAKING & THEORY OF MIND (EPISTEMIC)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Diva must reason about how another individual might understand a situation differently
from her own understanding. This is epistemic perspective-taking — modeling what
others know, believe, and expect.

4.1 Individual Knowledge Model:
    For each relevant individual, the engine maintains an estimated knowledge state:

    `IndividualKnowledgeModel`:
    - `model_id`: UUID.
    - `individual_id`: UUID (Foreign Key to IndividualProfile).
    - `known_facts`: Tuple of `KnowledgeFact` records (things this person likely knows).
    - `unknown_gaps`: Tuple of `KnowledgeGap` records (things this person likely
      does NOT know).
    - `possible_beliefs`: Tuple of `BeliefEstimate` records (what this person may believe).
    - `expected_expectations`: Tuple of `ExpectationEstimate` records.
    - `model_confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `last_updated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.

4.2 Epistemic Asymmetry Representation:
    The critical capability is representing that Diva and another individual have
    different information states:

    Example: Diva knows a project has failed. The other person does not know.
    - Diva's knowledge: {project_X: FAILED} (confidence 1.0, source OBSERVED).
    - Person_A's estimated knowledge: {project_X: IN_PROGRESS} (confidence 0.75,
      source INFERRED from last communication).
    - Epistemic gap: Person_A does NOT know about project_X failure.

    This asymmetry MUST be representable and must NOT be collapsed. Diva must not
    assume others share her knowledge state.

4.3 Belief Estimation:
    `BeliefEstimate` records model what another person may believe:
    - `belief_id`: UUID.
    - `individual_id`: UUID.
    - `belief_content`: String (natural language description of the estimated belief).
    - `belief_confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0] — how confident Diva is that the
      person holds this belief.
    - `truth_assessment`: Enum (`LIKELY_TRUE`, `LIKELY_FALSE`, `UNKNOWN`, `IRRELEVANT`).
      Diva's own assessment of whether the belief is actually true.
    - `evidence_basis`: Tuple of evidence record IDs.
    - `source_type`: Enum (`STATED_DIRECTLY`, `INFERRED_FROM_BEHAVIOR`,
      `INFERRED_FROM_CONTEXT`, `REPORTED_BY_THIRD_PARTY`).

    Important: Diva can recognize that an individual holds an incorrect belief
    without immediately correcting them. The decision to correct or not belongs
    to Cognitive Integration, not Social Cognition.

4.4 Emotional Perspective Estimation:
    The engine can estimate another individual's possible emotional state:
    - `estimated_emotion`: String (e.g., "frustrated", "excited", "anxious").
    - `estimation_confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `evidence_signals`: Tuple of evidence references.

    Critical Boundary: This is cognitive empathy — understanding what someone MIGHT
    feel. The Emotion Engine owns Diva's resulting empathetic emotional response.
    Social Cognition produces the assessment; Emotion Engine evaluates whether Diva
    feels empathy, compassion, concern, etc.

4.5 Perspective Update Protocol:
    Knowledge models and belief estimates are updated through interaction:
    1. After each interaction with an individual, check whether new information
       was shared that changes the estimated knowledge state.
    2. Apply Bayesian-style updating: new evidence shifts belief confidence up or down.
    3. Record the update in `social_model_history` for audit.
    4. Stale perspective models (not updated for $\ge 10$ interactions with the
       individual) are marked `STALE` and excluded from active context packages.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RELATIONSHIP MANAGEMENT & MULTIDIMENSIONAL REPRESENTATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Relationships are independent entities. An individual profile describes a person.
A relationship record describes Diva's relationship with that person.

5.1 Relationship Structure:
    Every relationship is represented by a `RelationshipRecord`:
    - `relationship_id`: UUID.
    - `individual_id`: UUID (Foreign Key to IndividualProfile).
    - `relationship_category`: Enum (`STRANGER`, `ACQUAINTANCE`, `FRIEND`,
      `CLOSE_FRIEND`, `FAMILY`, `COLLABORATOR`, `PROFESSIONAL_CONTACT`,
      `MENTOR`, `RIVAL`, `ROMANTIC_INTEREST`, `PARTNER`, `CUSTOM`).
    - `custom_category_label`: Optional String (for CUSTOM categories).
    - `category_confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `dimensions`: Immutable Tuple of `RelationshipDimension` records.
    - `status`: Enum (`FORMING`, `ACTIVE`, `STABLE`, `COOLING`, `DORMANT`,
      `RECOVERING`, `ENDED`).
    - `first_interaction_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
    - `last_interaction_at`: ISO-8601 UTC timestamp.
    - `total_interaction_count`: Integer.
    - `relationship_version`: Integer (monotonically increasing).

    Category Assignment Rules:
    - Initial category for new individuals: `STRANGER` (always).
    - Category promotion requires explicit evidence thresholds (defined below).
    - Categories are NEVER automatically assigned based solely on interaction count.
    - Multiple relationship categories are NOT supported simultaneously (one primary
      category). Instead, the multidimensional scores capture the nuance.

5.2 Eight Independent Relationship Dimensions:
    Each dimension is an independent measurement with its own update rules:

    1. FAMILIARITY ($F \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       How well Diva knows the individual.
       Update: Increases with interaction frequency and depth.
       Formula: $F_{new} = F_{old} + \alpha_F \cdot (1 - F_{old}) \cdot w_{interaction}$
       where $\alpha_F = 0.03$ (gradual familiarity growth) and
       $w_{interaction} \in [0.1, 1.0]$ weights interaction depth.
       Decay: $F(t) = F_0 \cdot \exp(-\lambda_F \cdot \Delta t_{days})$
       where $\lambda_F = 0.005$ (slow decay — familiarity fades slowly).

    2. TRUST ($T \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       Confidence based on observed behavioral evidence (commitments kept vs. broken).
       Update: Asymmetric — trust grows slowly through fulfilled commitments and
       decreases more rapidly through broken commitments.
       Growth: $T_{new} = T_{old} + \alpha_T^{+} \cdot (1 - T_{old}) \cdot w_{fulfillment}$
       where $\alpha_T^{+} = 0.02$ (trust builds slowly).
       Decline: $T_{new} = T_{old} - \alpha_T^{-} \cdot T_{old} \cdot w_{breach}$
       where $\alpha_T^{-} = 0.08$ (trust erodes faster than it builds).
       Recovery: After trust decline, recovery rate is halved:
       $\alpha_T^{recovery} = 0.01$ until $T$ exceeds the pre-breach value.

    3. AFFINITY ($A \in [-1.0, 1.0]$):
       Positive or negative interpersonal connection. Bidirectional scale:
       $A > 0$: Positive connection (liking, warmth).
       $A = 0$: Neutral.
       $A < 0$: Negative connection (dislike, aversion).
       Update: Influenced by interaction quality, shared interests, emotional resonance.

    4. COOPERATION ($Co \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       History of working together effectively.
       Update: Increases with successful collaborative outcomes, decreases with
       failed or unreciprocated collaboration attempts.

    5. COMMUNICATION ($Cm \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       Quality and consistency of interactions.
       Update: Tracks response reliability, conversational depth, mutual engagement.
       Formula accounts for both frequency and quality:
       $Cm_{new} = 0.6 \cdot Q_{recent} + 0.4 \cdot Cm_{old}$
       where $Q_{recent}$ is a quality assessment from the last $k$ interactions.

    6. RECIPROCITY ($Rp \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       Evidence of mutual engagement (not one-sided interaction).
       Update: Tracks the balance between Diva initiating vs. the other person
       initiating, and the balance of effort/disclosure in interactions.
       Formula: $Rp = 1.0 - |r_{Diva} - r_{other}|$
       where $r_{Diva}$ and $r_{other}$ are normalized initiation/effort ratios.

    7. CONFLICT ($Cf \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       Unresolved disagreements or negative experiences.
       Update: Increases with unresolved conflicts, decreases as conflicts are
       resolved or fade with time.
       Decay: Resolved conflicts decay at $\lambda_{Cf} = 0.01$ per interaction.
       Unresolved conflicts do NOT decay automatically.

    8. CLOSENESS ($Cl \in [0.0, 1.0]$):
       Degree of established interpersonal connection.
       This is a DERIVED dimension — not directly updated but computed from
       the other seven dimensions:
       $Cl = w_F \cdot F + w_T \cdot T + w_A \cdot \max(0, A) + w_{Co} \cdot Co
            + w_{Cm} \cdot Cm + w_{Rp} \cdot Rp - w_{Cf} \cdot Cf$
       Default weights: $w_F = 0.15, w_T = 0.20, w_A = 0.20, w_{Co} = 0.10,
       w_{Cm} = 0.15, w_{Rp} = 0.10, w_{Cf} = 0.10$.
       Clamped to [0.0, 1.0].

    Independence Principle:
    Every non-derived dimension updates independently. High familiarity does NOT
    automatically increase trust. High trust does NOT automatically increase affinity.
    Each dimension requires its own evidence stream.

5.3 Relationship Category Transition Thresholds:
    Categories transition based on dimension evidence, not arbitrary time:

    STRANGER -> ACQUAINTANCE:
    Requires: $F \ge 0.20$ AND total_interaction_count $\ge 3$.

    ACQUAINTANCE -> FRIEND:
    Requires: $F \ge 0.50$ AND $A \ge 0.30$ AND $Cm \ge 0.40$
    AND total_interaction_count $\ge 15$.

    FRIEND -> CLOSE_FRIEND:
    Requires: $F \ge 0.75$ AND $T \ge 0.65$ AND $A \ge 0.60$ AND $Cl \ge 0.65$
    AND interaction span $\ge 30$ days.

    COLLABORATOR (from any state $\ge$ ACQUAINTANCE):
    Requires: $Co \ge 0.50$ AND $\ge 5$ documented cooperative interactions.

    Reverse transitions (relationship cooling):
    If $Cm$ drops below the entry threshold for 20+ interactions: Consider downgrade.
    Downgrade is NEVER automatic — it creates a `RelationshipTransitionProposal`
    that is logged and applied only after validation.

5.4 Relationship History:
    Every dimension update and category transition is recorded in an append-only
    `relationship_history` table:
    - `history_id`: UUID.
    - `relationship_id`: UUID.
    - `event_type`: Enum (`DIMENSION_UPDATE`, `CATEGORY_TRANSITION`, `STATUS_CHANGE`,
      `EVIDENCE_ADDED`, `CORRECTION`).
    - `dimension_name`: Optional String.
    - `previous_value`: Optional Float.
    - `new_value`: Optional Float.
    - `evidence_id`: Optional UUID.
    - `timestamp`: ISO-8601 UTC.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SOCIAL INTERACTION INTERPRETATION & CONTEXTUAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine must evaluate social interactions using full available context,
maintaining multiple interpretations for ambiguous situations.

6.1 Interaction Analysis Pipeline:
    For each socially significant interaction:
    1. Perception Engine delivers the processed interaction content and metadata.
    2. Identity Manager resolves the interlocutor identity.
    3. Social Context Manager retrieves relevant individual profile, relationship
       record, and recent interaction history.
    4. Social Interaction Analyzer generates a structured `InteractionAssessment`:

    `InteractionAssessment`:
    - `assessment_id`: UUID.
    - `interaction_id`: UUID.
    - `interlocutor_id`: UUID.
    - `interaction_type`: Enum (`CONVERSATIONAL`, `COOPERATIVE`, `CONFLICT`,
      `REQUEST`, `DISCLOSURE`, `BOUNDARY_EVENT`, `GREETING`, `FAREWELL`,
      `AMBIGUOUS`).
    - `interpretations`: Tuple of `SocialInterpretation` records.
    - `relationship_implications`: Tuple of dimension update proposals.
    - `memory_significance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0] (nomination weight for Memory).
    - `emotional_relevance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0] (signal weight for Emotion Engine).
    - `assessed_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

6.2 Multi-Interpretation Preservation:
    For ambiguous interactions, the analyzer generates multiple `SocialInterpretation`
    records ranked by confidence:

    `SocialInterpretation`:
    - `interpretation_id`: UUID.
    - `assessment_id`: UUID.
    - `description`: String (natural language explanation).
    - `confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `supporting_context`: Tuple of evidence references.
    - `status`: Enum (`ACTIVE`, `SUPERSEDED`, `CONFIRMED`, `REFUTED`).

    Rule: When an interaction has $\ge 2$ plausible interpretations with the
    highest confidence $< 0.80$, ALL interpretations with confidence $\ge 0.15$
    are preserved as ACTIVE. The engine does NOT force premature resolution.

    Example — Non-response analysis:
    Someone doesn't respond to Diva for three days. Generated interpretations:
    1. "They are busy" — confidence 0.45 (most common explanation).
    2. "They haven't seen the message" — confidence 0.25.
    3. "They communicate infrequently" — confidence 0.15 (if pattern matches history).
    4. "They are avoiding the conversation" — confidence 0.10.
    None of these are automatically selected as "the" explanation.

6.3 Reinterpretation Protocol:
    Earlier interaction assessments can be reinterpreted when new evidence arrives:
    1. New evidence triggers a check against active interpretations for recent
       interactions with the same individual.
    2. If new evidence strongly supports one interpretation ($\Delta C \ge +0.20$):
       Update confidence values, potentially promoting one and demoting others.
    3. If new evidence contradicts the highest-confidence interpretation: Reduce
       its confidence and elevate alternatives.
    4. Record the reinterpretation event in interaction history with provenance.

6.4 Conversational Role Identification:
    The analyzer identifies the roles each participant plays in an interaction:
    - Initiator vs. Responder.
    - Information provider vs. Information seeker.
    - Helper vs. Helpee.
    - Leader vs. Follower (in group interactions).
    - Mediator (in multi-party conflicts).

    Roles are attached to `InteractionRecord` with per-interaction scope (someone
    may be a leader in one conversation and a follower in another).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SOCIAL NORMS, EXPECTATIONS & CONTEXTUAL CONVENTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Social behavior depends on context. The engine must maintain flexible, context-
dependent representations of social norms.

7.1 Social Norm Representation:
    `SocialNormRecord`:
    - `norm_id`: UUID.
    - `norm_category`: Enum (`CONVERSATIONAL_ETIQUETTE`, `PERSONAL_BOUNDARY`,
      `PROFESSIONAL_BOUNDARY`, `CULTURAL_CONVENTION`, `COMMUNITY_RULE`,
      `PRIVACY_EXPECTATION`, `RECIPROCITY_EXPECTATION`, `CUSTOM`).
    - `description`: String (natural language description of the norm).
    - `applicable_contexts`: Tuple of context tags.
    - `source_type`: Enum (`OBSERVED_PATTERN`, `EXPLICITLY_STATED`,
      `CULTURALLY_GENERAL`, `INDIVIDUAL_PREFERENCE`).
    - `confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `evidence_count`: Integer.
    - `exceptions`: Tuple of known exceptions.

7.2 Context-Dependent Norm Application:
    The same behavior may be appropriate in one context and inappropriate in another:
    - Communication style varies between close friends, professional contacts,
      and strangers.
    - Response time expectations differ between urgent professional communication
      and casual social interaction.
    - Disclosure norms differ between private one-on-one conversations and
      group/public settings.

    The Social Norms Manager maintains a context taxonomy:
    - `INTIMATE`: Close friends, family, romantic partners.
    - `CASUAL`: Friends, acquaintances in informal settings.
    - `PROFESSIONAL`: Work colleagues, clients, formal interactions.
    - `PUBLIC`: Group settings, community interactions.
    - `ANONYMOUS`: Unknown or unidentified interlocutors.

7.3 Individual vs. General Norms:
    The engine distinguishes between:
    - General social norms (applicable broadly).
    - Individual preferences (specific to one person).
    When an individual's stated preference conflicts with a general norm, the
    individual preference takes priority for interactions with that individual.

7.4 Norm Learning:
    Social norms are learned through experience:
    - Explicit statements about preferences ("I prefer if you don't bring that up").
    - Repeated patterns of behavior across interactions.
    - Corrections and feedback ("That was too formal for our conversation").
    - Cultural context signals.

    Learning Rate: New norms from explicit statements are recorded immediately
    with confidence 0.70. Norms from repeated patterns require $\ge 3$ consistent
    observations for $C \ge 0.50$.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. SOCIAL TRUST & RELIABILITY ASSESSMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Trust is based on accumulated evidence, not positive feelings. Trust and affection
are independent. The engine maintains contextual, evidence-based trust assessments.

8.1 Trust Assessment Structure:
    `TrustAssessment`:
    - `assessment_id`: UUID.
    - `individual_id`: UUID.
    - `context`: Enum (`GENERAL`, `TECHNICAL`, `PERSONAL`, `PROFESSIONAL`,
      `EMOTIONAL`, `FINANCIAL`, `CUSTOM`).
    - `overall_trust`: Float in [0.0, 1.0] (computed from evidence).
    - `fulfilled_commitments`: Integer.
    - `broken_commitments`: Integer.
    - `statement_action_consistency`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `evidence_references`: Tuple of evidence record IDs.
    - `assessment_version`: Integer.
    - `last_updated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

8.2 Contextual Trust:
    Trust is assessed per-context:
    - Someone might be reliable for technical collaboration but unreliable for
      personal commitments.
    - Each context maintains its own evidence stream and trust score.
    - General trust is a weighted average of contextual trust scores:
      $T_{general} = \sum_{c} w_c \cdot T_c / \sum_{c} w_c$
      where weights reflect evidence volume per context.

8.3 Trust Update Algorithm:
    Trust updates follow an asymmetric Bayesian-inspired model:

    Commitment Fulfillment:
    $T_{new} = T_{old} + \alpha^{+} \cdot (1 - T_{old}) \cdot w_{significance}$
    where $\alpha^{+} = 0.03$ and $w_{significance} \in [0.1, 1.0]$ weights the
    importance of the fulfilled commitment.

    Commitment Breach:
    $T_{new} = T_{old} - \alpha^{-} \cdot T_{old} \cdot w_{severity}$
    where $\alpha^{-} = 0.10$ and $w_{severity} \in [0.1, 1.0]$ weights the
    severity of the breach.

    Consistency Check:
    Statement-action consistency is tracked as a running ratio:
    $SAC = \frac{n_{consistent}}{n_{consistent} + n_{inconsistent}}$
    Low SAC ($< 0.50$) triggers a trust reduction modifier.

    Anti-Single-Incident Rule:
    A single isolated negative incident NEVER reduces trust by more than $0.10$.
    Severe trust reduction requires $\ge 2$ negative evidence instances.

8.4 Trust Recovery:
    After trust deterioration:
    - Recovery rate is halved compared to initial trust building:
      $\alpha_{recovery} = \alpha^{+} / 2 = 0.015$.
    - Recovery requires consistent positive evidence across $\ge 5$ interactions.
    - Full recovery to pre-breach level is possible but requires proportionally
      more evidence than the original trust building.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. SOCIAL LEARNING & BEHAVIORAL PATTERN RECOGNITION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine must identify recurring patterns across interactions and submit
appropriate learning signals to the Learning Engine.

9.1 Pattern Detection Categories:
    The Social Pattern Recognition Module identifies patterns in:
    - Communication patterns (frequency, style, timing).
    - Behavioral consistency (reliable vs. erratic behavior).
    - Cooperation patterns (collaborative success/failure rates).
    - Conflict patterns (recurring disagreement topics or escalation patterns).
    - Relationship trajectory patterns (developing, stable, declining).
    - Cross-individual patterns (similar behaviors across different individuals).

9.2 Pattern Evidence Requirements:
    A pattern is NOT identified from a single instance:
    - Minimum observations: $n_{pattern} \ge 3$ consistent instances.
    - Minimum span: Observations must span $\ge 2$ distinct interactions.
    - Pattern confidence: $C_{pattern} = \frac{n_{supporting}}{n_{supporting} + n_{contradicting}} \cdot \min(1.0, n_{supporting} / 5)$
    The second factor ensures that even a 100% hit rate on 2 observations does not
    produce full confidence — volume matters.

9.3 Pattern Lifecycle:
    - `EMERGING`: $3 \le n_{supporting} \le 5$, confidence preliminary.
    - `ESTABLISHED`: $n_{supporting} > 5$, confidence $\ge 0.65$.
    - `FADING`: No new supporting evidence for $\ge 10$ interactions.
    - `CONTRADICTED`: Recent contradictory evidence exceeds supporting evidence.

9.4 Learning Engine Integration:
    The Social Learning Interface dispatches `SocialAttunementSignal` to the
    Learning Engine (contract established in Learning Engine v2.5.0):

    `SocialAttunementSignal`:
    - `signal_id`: UUID.
    - `signal_type`: Enum (`COMMUNICATIVE_MISALIGNMENT`, `RAPPORT_SHIFT`,
      `PATTERN_DISCOVERY`, `TRUST_EVIDENCE`, `RELATIONSHIP_TRANSITION`,
      `NORM_VIOLATION_DETECTED`).
    - `affected_parameter_domain`: String (e.g., `social.conversational_verbosity`,
      `social.empathy_weight`, `social.trust_initial_baseline`).
    - `evidence_summary`: Structured evidence payload.
    - `suggested_direction`: Enum (`INCREASE`, `DECREASE`, `RECALIBRATE`).
    - `evidence_strength`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].

    Ownership Boundary: Social Cognition dispatches signals. Learning Engine
    independently evaluates whether the evidence warrants a parameter change.
    If Learning Engine approves, it dispatches a `ParameterChangeRequest` back
    to Social Cognition (or whichever engine owns the target parameter). Social
    Cognition, as owner of `social.*` parameters, validates and applies changes
    with version-guarded optimistic concurrency.

10. SOCIAL CONTEXT & MEMORY INTEGRATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The engine maintains connections between individuals, relationships, and
experiences without duplicating Memory's permanent storage role.

10.1 Social Context Package:
    When other engines need social context, the Social Context Manager assembles
    a structured `SocialContextPackage`:

    `SocialContextPackage`:
    - `package_id`: UUID.
    - `requesting_engine`: Enum.
    - `interlocutor_profile`: Frozen IndividualProfile snapshot.
    - `relationship_summary`: Frozen RelationshipRecord snapshot.
    - `recent_interactions`: Tuple of recent InteractionRecord summaries.
    - `active_interpretations`: Tuple of active SocialInterpretation records.
    - `active_intention_hypotheses`: Tuple of active IntentionHypothesis records.
    - `relevant_norms`: Tuple of applicable SocialNormRecord entries.
    - `trust_assessment`: Frozen TrustAssessment snapshot.
    - `group_context`: Optional GroupProfile snapshot.
    - `overall_confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `assembled_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

10.2 Memory Integration Protocol:
    Memory owns permanent storage. Social Cognition owns structured interpretation.

    Social Cognition -> Memory:
    - Dispatches `MemoryNomination` for significant social experiences.
    - Nomination criteria: Interaction significance $\ge 0.60$ OR relationship
      transition events OR trust-relevant evidence.
    - Nominations include structured metadata (individual_id, relationship context).

    Memory -> Social Cognition:
    - Provides historical interaction records via `MemoryContextProvider`.
    - Provides individual-associated memories for context enrichment.
    - Returns canonical memory IDs for provenance linkage.

    Boundary: Social Cognition does NOT duplicate Memory's storage. It maintains
    working models that reference Memory records via canonical IDs. When a social
    model needs reconstruction (e.g., after dormancy), Social Cognition retrieves
    historical data from Memory and rebuilds the working model.

10.3 Social Model Reconstruction:
    When an individual re-appears after dormancy:
    1. Retrieve associated memories from Memory Engine using the individual_id.
    2. Reconstruct a working IndividualProfile from historical evidence.
    3. Reconstruct relationship dimensions from stored history.
    4. Mark reconstructed attributes with reduced confidence ($C_{reconstructed} = C_{original} \cdot 0.70$).
    5. Resume active modeling from the reconstructed state.

12. SOCIAL UNCERTAINTY, CORRECTION & PROVENANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a foundational architectural requirement, not an optional feature.

12.1 Confidence Classification Taxonomy:
    Every piece of social information carries:
    - `evidence_type`: Enum (`FACT`, `STATEMENT`, `ASSUMPTION`, `HYPOTHESIS`).
    - `confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `evidence_provenance`: Tuple of `EvidenceProvenance` records.

    `EvidenceProvenance`:
    - `provenance_id`: UUID.
    - `source_type`: Enum (`DIRECT_OBSERVATION`, `SELF_REPORT`, `THIRD_PARTY_REPORT`,
      `BEHAVIORAL_INFERENCE`, `CONTEXTUAL_INFERENCE`, `LLM_INTERPRETATION`).
    - `source_individual_id`: Optional UUID (who provided the information).
    - `source_interaction_id`: Optional UUID (which interaction).
    - `recorded_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

12.2 Self-Correction Protocol:
    Individuals can correct information about themselves:
    1. The correction is recorded as a `SocialEvidenceRecord` with type
       `SELF_CORRECTION` and highest source reliability.
    2. The corrected attribute's evidence classification is updated.
    3. Previous incorrect information is NOT deleted but marked as `SUPERSEDED`
       with a reference to the correction event.
    4. If the correction contradicts multiple inferred attributes, all affected
       attributes are re-evaluated.

12.3 Historical Version Preservation:
    Major social model changes are version-tracked:
    - Relationship category transitions.
    - Trust assessment revisions $> 0.15$.
    - Identity corrections (merges, splits).
    - Belief model revisions.
    Each version is recorded in `social_model_history` with the previous and new
    state, the triggering evidence, and the timestamp.

12.4 Anti-Speculation-to-Fact Safeguard:
    The system enforces a hard boundary between speculative and established
    information:
    - HYPOTHESIZED information ($C < 0.30$) is NEVER included in Social Context
      Packages sent to other engines without explicit uncertainty markers.
    - INFERRED information ($0.30 \le C < 0.70$) is included with mandatory
      confidence values and evidence_type classification.
    - Only OBSERVED or high-confidence INFERRED ($C \ge 0.70$) information
      is presented without additional qualification.

13. SHARED LLM INTEGRATION & DETERMINISTIC BOUNDARIES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Social Cognition Engine uses the shared LLM orchestration layer. It does NOT
own an independent model instance.

13.1 Tasks Delegated to LLM:
    - Understanding complex social situations.
    - Interpreting indirect communication (subtext, implication, sarcasm).
    - Generating possible explanations for observed behavior.
    - Reasoning about another individual's perspective.
    - Identifying nuanced social patterns across interactions.
    - Interpreting ambiguous interpersonal situations.
    - Estimating emotional perspectives of other individuals.
    - Understanding cultural context signals.

    LLM Request Structure:
    `SocialLLMRequest`:
    - `request_id`: UUID.
    - `request_type`: Enum (`INTENTION_ANALYSIS`, `PERSPECTIVE_INFERENCE`,
      `INTERACTION_INTERPRETATION`, `PATTERN_ANALYSIS`, `NORM_INTERPRETATION`,
      `INDIRECT_COMMUNICATION`, `EMOTIONAL_PERSPECTIVE`).
    - `context_payload`: Structured context data (individual profile, interaction
      history, relationship state).
    - `constraints`: Tuple of interpretation constraints.
    - `requested_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

    LLM Response Handling:
    `SocialLLMResponse`:
    - `response_id`: UUID.
    - `request_id`: UUID (links to request).
    - `interpretations`: Tuple of scored interpretations.
    - `confidence_self_assessment`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
    - `processing_time_ms`: Integer.

    Post-LLM Validation:
    Every LLM response is validated by the deterministic layer before persistence:
    1. Confidence values must be in valid range [0.0, 1.0].
    2. Interpretations must reference existing evidence records.
    3. No interpretation may assert certainty ($C = 1.0$) about inferred information.
    4. Deception hypotheses must NOT be the sole or highest-confidence output
       without strong contradictory evidence.
    5. Responses exceeding timeout ($T_{timeout} = 10,000$ ms) are discarded.

13.2 Tasks Remaining Deterministic:
    The following operations NEVER involve LLM calls:
    - Identity record creation and UUID assignment.
    - Identifier string matching and validation.
    - Relationship history append operations.
    - Interaction timestamp recording.
    - Evidence counting and aggregation.
    - Confidence value arithmetic (formula application).
    - Dimension score updates (formula application).
    - Relationship category threshold checks.
    - Version incrementation.
    - Data persistence (SQLite transactions).
    - Duplicate record prevention (unique constraint enforcement).
    - Privacy and access policy enforcement.

14. SOCIAL MODEL LIFECYCLE, STALENESS & GARBAGE COLLECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Social models have a defined lifecycle independent of Memory's historical storage.

14.1 Social Model Lifecycle States:
    `ModelLifecycleState`:
    - `ACTIVE`: Frequently relevant person; model updated regularly.
    - `COOLING`: Interaction frequency declining; model maintained but
      update frequency reduced.
    - `DORMANT`: Not recently relevant; model retained in compressed form.
    - `ARCHIVED`: Working model removed from active memory. Historical data
      retained in Memory Engine. Can be reconstructed on re-encounter.

    State Transition Rules:
    ACTIVE -> COOLING: No interaction for $> 30$ days (configurable).
    COOLING -> DORMANT: No interaction for $> 90$ days (configurable).
    DORMANT -> ARCHIVED: No interaction for $> 180$ days AND social relevance
    score $< 0.20$ (configurable).
    Any -> ACTIVE: New interaction with the individual.
    ARCHIVED -> ACTIVE: Triggered by re-encounter; requires model reconstruction
    from Memory Engine.

14.2 Social Relevance Score:
    Social relevance determines model priority and lifecycle transitions:
    $R_{social} = w_{freq} \cdot f_{interaction} + w_{recency} \cdot r_{last}
                 + w_{personality} \cdot p_{affinity} + w_{relationship} \cdot d_{closeness}
                 + w_{memory} \cdot m_{frequency}$

    Where:
    - $f_{interaction}$: Normalized interaction frequency.
    - $r_{last}$: Recency factor (exponential decay from last interaction).
    - $p_{affinity}$: Personality Engine's social affinity signal for this individual.
    - $d_{closeness}$: Closeness dimension value from relationship record.
    - $m_{frequency}$: Memory Engine's frequency of this person in stored memories.

    Default weights: $w_{freq} = 0.25, w_{recency} = 0.30, w_{personality} = 0.15,
    w_{relationship} = 0.20, w_{memory} = 0.10$.

14.3 Garbage Collection Rules:
    - Model archival does NOT erase historical memories about the person.
    - If the person re-appears after archival, Social Cognition reconstructs from
      Memory Engine data.
    - Archived models retain only: individual_id, known identifiers, last
      relationship category, and last interaction timestamp.
    - Full model data is recoverable from Memory Engine + archived summary.

14.4 Privacy & Retention Policy:
    - Information explicitly shared by an individual is tagged as `SHARED_BY_SUBJECT`.
    - Inferences made by Diva are tagged as `DIVA_INFERENCE`.
    - If an individual requests information removal: `SHARED_BY_SUBJECT` items
      are marked for deletion. `DIVA_INFERENCE` items are marked for review.
    - Retention periods are configurable per information category.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
15. RESOURCE THROTTLING, LIFECYCLE STATES & TRANSACTIONAL PERSISTENCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

15.1 Engine Lifecycle States:
    The Social Cognition Engine operates in one of:
    - `INITIALIZING`: Loading persisted state from SQLite.
    - `ACTIVE`: Fully operational, processing social information.
    - `THROTTLED`: Reduced processing due to low cognitive resources.
    - `SUSPENDED`: Paused by Internal State Engine; no active processing.
    - `RECOVERING`: Post-crash state reconstruction.

15.2 Resource-Aware Processing:
    When Internal State Engine reports low cognitive resources:
    - Throttle LLM interpretation requests (increase minimum significance threshold).
    - Defer non-urgent model updates (pattern analysis, norm learning).
    - Prioritize identity resolution and active interaction interpretation.
    - Pause background social model maintenance (staleness checks, garbage collection).

15.3 Crash Recovery Protocol:
    1. Reload all persisted state from SQLite (identity records, profiles,
       relationships, trust assessments).
    2. Verify referential integrity across all foreign key relationships.
    3. Identify any incomplete transactions (pending dimension updates,
       in-progress identity merges).
    4. Roll back incomplete transactions to last consistent state.
    5. Reconstruct in-memory indexes (identity lookup, relationship lookup).
    6. Mark engine as ACTIVE.
    7. Resume processing from the latest persisted interaction timestamp.

15.4 SQLite Transactional Boundaries:
    Atomic operations (single transaction):
    - Identity creation + initial profile + initial relationship record.
    - Dimension update + history record + evidence linkage.
    - Identity merge/split + all affected reference updates.
    - Pattern creation + evidence linkage.
    - Trust assessment update + evidence record.

    Non-atomic (may span multiple transactions):
    - LLM interpretation request and response handling.
    - Multi-individual group dynamics analysis.
    - Background staleness and garbage collection sweeps.


================================================================================
PART 2 — INTERNAL ARCHITECTURE & THE THIRTEEN LOGICAL COMPONENTS
================================================================================

The Social Cognition Engine is structured into thirteen cooperating, event-driven
logical components. Each component is defined with explicit inputs, outputs,
internal state, data structures, algorithms, and execution timing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. INDIVIDUAL IDENTITY MANAGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Recognizes, registers, disambiguates, and maintains individual identities
across all interactions.

- Inputs:
  * Perception Engine: Interaction metadata (account IDs, platform identifiers,
    display names, behavioral signals).
  * Other engines: Entity references requiring identity resolution.
  * User corrections: Explicit identity corrections.

- Outputs:
  * Resolved `individual_id` for each interaction participant.
  * `IdentityResolutionResult` with confidence and alternative matches.
  * Identity merge/split events dispatched to all dependent components.

- Internal State:
  * In-memory identity index: HashMap<identifier_string, List<individual_id>>.
  * In-memory behavioral signature cache: HashMap<individual_id, BehavioralSignature>.
  * Active identity uncertainty flags.

- Algorithms:
  * Three-stage identity resolution:
    Stage 1 — Explicit Identifier Match: O(1) lookup in identifier index.
    Stage 2 — Behavioral Similarity (if Stage 1 fails): LLM-assisted comparison
    of communication patterns, vocabulary, and interaction style against recent
    profiles. Cost: O(k) where k = number of active profiles checked.
    Stage 3 — New Identity Registration: If no match found, create new profile.
  * Anti-merge validation: Before any merge, check for contradictory evidence
    (simultaneous interactions, conflicting personal details).
  * Anti-duplication check: Before creation, similarity scan against existing profiles.

- Decision Rules:
  * Merge threshold: Explicit identifier match AND behavioral similarity $\ge 0.75$
    AND no contradictory evidence.
  * Split trigger: Explicit correction OR strong contradictory evidence across
    attributed interactions.

- Error Handling:
  * Ambiguous identity: Create tentative profile with $C_{id} < 0.60$.
  * Failed LLM behavioral analysis: Fall back to identifier-only matching.
  * Merge conflict: Abort merge, create `PossibleIdentityLink` for manual review.

- Execution Timing: Synchronous on every interaction ingress. Identity must be
  resolved before any other component processes the interaction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. INDIVIDUAL MODELING MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Builds and maintains structured models of each individual through
accumulated, evidence-attributed observations.

- Inputs:
  * Identity Manager: Resolved individual_id.
  * Interaction content and metadata from Perception Engine.
  * LLM interpretation results (attributed observations).
  * Memory Engine: Historical context for the individual.

- Outputs:
  * Updated IndividualProfile with versioned attributes.
  * Attribute change notifications to Relationship Manager and Trust Assessment.
  * Stale attribute notifications to Social Lifecycle Manager.

- Internal State:
  * In-memory profile cache: HashMap<individual_id, IndividualProfile>.
  * Attribute evidence accumulation buffers.

- Algorithms:
  * Four-Level Evidence Attribution assignment (OBSERVED, REPORTED, INFERRED,
    HYPOTHESIZED) using source analysis.
  * Attribute confidence update: Bayesian-style accumulation.
    $C_{new} = \frac{C_{old} \cdot n_{old} + C_{evidence} \cdot w_{evidence}}{n_{old} + w_{evidence}}$
    where $w_{evidence}$ is the weight of the new evidence based on its type.
  * Contradiction detection: Compare new evidence against existing attributes
    with same semantic domain. If polarity differs, trigger contradiction protocol.
  * Staleness decay: $R(t) = R_0 \cdot \exp(-0.02 \cdot \Delta t_{interactions})$.
  * Promotion/demotion rule engine: Check evidence counts against thresholds
    for evidence level transitions.

- Decision Rules:
  * Never convert a single casual statement into a permanent personality attribute.
  * Require $\ge 3$ supporting instances for HYPOTHESIZED -> INFERRED promotion.
  * Require $\ge 5$ direct observations across $\ge 3$ interactions for OBSERVED status.

- Error Handling:
  * Missing individual_id: Reject update, log error.
  * Attribute validation failure: Reject attribute, preserve existing state.
  * Concurrent updates to same profile: Version-guarded optimistic concurrency;
    retry on version mismatch.

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous, triggered after interaction analysis completion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. INTENTION INFERENCE MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Generates structured hypotheses about other individuals' possible
intentions, goals, and motivations without treating hypotheses as facts.

- Inputs:
  * Interaction content and metadata.
  * Individual profile context.
  * Relationship context.
  * LLM interpretation of communication intent.

- Outputs:
  * Ranked set of `IntentionHypothesis` records per significant interaction.
  * `IndividualGoalEstimate` for sustained patterns across interactions.
  * Hypothesis status updates (CONFIRMED, REFUTED, SUPERSEDED).

- Internal State:
  * Active hypothesis cache: HashMap<individual_id, List<IntentionHypothesis>>.
  * Longitudinal goal tracking buffer.

- Algorithms:
  * Multi-hypothesis generation: For each significant interaction, produce
    $\ge 2$ hypotheses. LLM generates candidates; deterministic layer scores
    and validates them.
  * Confidence decay: $C(t) = C_0 \cdot 0.90^{n_{interactions}}$ per hypothesis.
  * Longitudinal aggregation: When $\ge 3$ interaction-level hypotheses of the
    same type exceed $C \ge 0.50$, create an IndividualGoalEstimate.
  * Deception safeguard: DECEPTION_POSSIBLE never generated as sole/highest
    hypothesis without $\ge 2$ contradictory evidence instances.

- Decision Rules:
  * Ambiguous interaction ($\max(C_{hypotheses}) < 0.80$): Preserve all hypotheses
    with $C \ge 0.15$.
  * Clear interaction ($\max(C_{hypotheses}) \ge 0.80$): Primary hypothesis leads,
    alternatives retained at reduced weight.

- Error Handling:
  * LLM failure: Fall back to pattern-based intention estimation from historical
    interaction similarity.
  * Confidence overflow: Clamp all values to [0.0, 1.0].

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous, triggered after interaction analysis.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PERSPECTIVE MODELING MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Represents other individuals' possible beliefs, knowledge states,
expectations, and emotional perspectives — enabling epistemic perspective-taking.

- Inputs:
  * Individual profile and interaction history.
  * Diva's current knowledge state (from Thought Engine).
  * LLM perspective analysis results.

- Outputs:
  * IndividualKnowledgeModel updates.
  * BeliefEstimate records.
  * Epistemic asymmetry alerts (Diva knows X, Person likely doesn't).
  * Emotional perspective estimates dispatched to Emotion Engine.

- Internal State:
  * Active knowledge models: HashMap<individual_id, IndividualKnowledgeModel>.
  * Belief estimate cache.

- Algorithms:
  * Knowledge state inference: For each piece of information Diva possesses,
    estimate whether the interlocutor likely shares that knowledge based on:
    - What was communicated in prior interactions.
    - What is publicly available vs. privately known.
    - Temporal factors (information shared before vs. after the person's last interaction).
  * Belief updating: Bayesian confidence adjustment based on new interaction evidence.
  * Perspective divergence detection: When Diva's knowledge and the estimated
    knowledge of the other person diverge significantly on a topic relevant to
    the current interaction, generate an epistemic asymmetry alert.

- Decision Rules:
  * Never assume others share Diva's knowledge state.
  * Belief estimates with $C < 0.30$ are classified as HYPOTHESIZED.
  * Emotional perspective estimates are ALWAYS dispatched with explicit uncertainty.

- Error Handling:
  * Stale perspective models ($> 10$ interactions without update): Mark STALE,
    exclude from active context packages.
  * Contradictory belief evidence: Maintain both beliefs with updated confidences.

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous, triggered by significant interaction events
  or explicit perspective queries from other engines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RELATIONSHIP MANAGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Maintains interpersonal relationships as independent entities with
multidimensional representation and versioned history.

- Inputs:
  * Interaction assessments from Social Interaction Analyzer.
  * Trust assessments from Trust Assessment Module.
  * Individual profile updates from Individual Modeling Module.
  * Cooperation and reciprocity signals from interaction analysis.

- Outputs:
  * Updated RelationshipRecord with current dimension values.
  * RelationshipTransitionProposal for category changes.
  * Relationship dimension snapshots for Social Context Packages.
  * Relationship history records (append-only).

- Internal State:
  * In-memory relationship index: HashMap<individual_id, RelationshipRecord>.
  * Dimension update buffers (batch updates within a single interaction cycle).

- Algorithms:
  * Independent dimension update formulas (Section 5.2 of Part 1).
  * Closeness derivation: Weighted combination of seven independent dimensions.
  * Category transition threshold checking against evidence-based criteria.
  * History recording for every dimension change $> 0.01$.

- Decision Rules:
  * Category changes require threshold satisfaction AND validation.
  * Reverse transitions (cooling) are NEVER automatic — always create a proposal.
  * Dimension updates are independent — updating one NEVER automatically updates another.

- Error Handling:
  * Missing individual_id reference: Reject update.
  * Version conflict: Retry with fresh read.
  * Relationship record not found: Create initial STRANGER relationship.

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous, triggered after interaction assessment completion.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SOCIAL INTERACTION ANALYZER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Interprets social interactions using full available context, generating
multi-interpretation assessments.

- Inputs:
  * Perception Engine: Processed interaction content and metadata.
  * Identity Manager: Resolved interlocutor identity.
  * Social Context Manager: Relevant individual profile, relationship, and history.
  * LLM interpretation results.

- Outputs:
  * InteractionAssessment with ranked interpretations.
  * Dimension update proposals to Relationship Manager.
  * Memory nomination signals for significant interactions.
  * Emotional relevance signals to Emotion Engine.

- Internal State:
  * Recent interaction buffer (last 20 interactions for reinterpretation checks).

- Algorithms:
  * Multi-interpretation generation using LLM + contextual priors.
  * Significance scoring: $S_{interaction} = w_{novelty} \cdot N + w_{emotional} \cdot E
    + w_{relational} \cdot R + w_{trust} \cdot T$
    where N = novelty of content, E = emotional intensity, R = relationship
    relevance, T = trust-relevant evidence presence.
  * Reinterpretation trigger: When new evidence shifts confidence of a recent
    interpretation by $\ge 0.20$.

- Execution Timing: Synchronous on every significant interaction ingress (after
  identity resolution).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. SOCIAL NORMS MANAGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Maintains context-dependent social norms, conventions, and expectations.

- Inputs:
  * Interaction patterns and feedback.
  * Individual preference statements.
  * Cultural and contextual signals from interactions.

- Outputs:
  * Applicable norm sets for Social Context Packages.
  * Norm violation signals for Social Learning Interface.
  * Individual preference overrides for specific interactions.

- Internal State:
  * Norm registry: HashMap<norm_category, List<SocialNormRecord>>.
  * Individual preference index: HashMap<individual_id, List<NormOverride>>.

- Algorithms:
  * Norm learning: Explicit statements recorded at $C = 0.70$.
  * Pattern-based norm discovery: $\ge 3$ consistent observations for $C \ge 0.50$.
  * Context matching: Select applicable norms based on relationship category,
    interaction context, and individual preferences.
  * Individual override priority: Individual-specific norms take precedence over
    general norms when both apply.

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous, queried by Social Context Manager on demand.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. TRUST ASSESSMENT MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Evaluates evidence-based, contextual trust assessments.

- Inputs:
  * Commitment tracking data (fulfilled vs. broken).
  * Statement-action consistency data.
  * Behavioral pattern evidence.
  * Individual profile reliability indicators.

- Outputs:
  * Updated TrustAssessment records.
  * Trust change signals to Relationship Manager (affects Trust dimension).
  * Trust-relevant evidence to Social Learning Interface.

- Internal State:
  * Active trust assessment cache: HashMap<(individual_id, context), TrustAssessment>.
  * Commitment tracking ledger.

- Algorithms:
  * Asymmetric trust update (Section 8.3 of Part 1).
  * Contextual trust separation (per-domain trust scores).
  * General trust derivation (weighted average of contextual scores).
  * Statement-action consistency ratio tracking.
  * Anti-single-incident safeguard enforcement.

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous, triggered by trust-relevant interaction events.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. SOCIAL PATTERN RECOGNITION MODULE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Identifies recurring patterns across interactions with individuals and groups.

- Inputs:
  * Interaction history from Social Context Manager.
  * Individual behavioral data from Individual Modeling Module.
  * Relationship trajectory data from Relationship Manager.

- Outputs:
  * SocialPatternRecord entries with lifecycle status.
  * Pattern discovery signals to Social Learning Interface.
  * Pattern-based predictions for Social Context Packages.

- Internal State:
  * Pattern candidate buffer: Accumulates observations until threshold.
  * Established pattern index: HashMap<individual_id, List<SocialPatternRecord>>.

- Algorithms:
  * Pattern detection: Sliding window analysis over interaction history.
  * Pattern confidence: $C_{pattern} = \frac{n_{supporting}}{n_{supporting} + n_{contradicting}} \cdot \min(1.0, n_{supporting} / 5)$.
  * Pattern lifecycle transitions: EMERGING -> ESTABLISHED -> FADING -> CONTRADICTED.
  * Cross-individual pattern detection: Identify similar patterns across different
    individuals (e.g., "people who discuss topic X tend to prefer brief responses").

- Execution Timing: Background periodic analysis (every 10 interactions or on
  significant relationship transitions).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. GROUP DYNAMICS MANAGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Maintains group and community models, tracks group membership, and
understands multi-party social dynamics.

- Inputs:
  * Multi-party interaction data.
  * Individual membership evidence.
  * Group-level behavioral observations.

- Outputs:
  * GroupProfile updates.
  * GroupMembership records.
  * Group dynamics snapshots for Social Context Packages.

- Internal State:
  * Group registry: HashMap<group_id, GroupProfile>.
  * Membership index: HashMap<individual_id, List<group_id>>.

- Algorithms:
  * Group discovery: Identify groups from co-occurrence patterns of individuals
    in interactions. Requires $\ge 3$ individuals co-occurring in $\ge 2$
    distinct interactions for group formation.
  * Membership evidence accumulation.
  * Group opinion vs. individual opinion discrimination.
  * Group dynamics assessment (cohesion, activity, tensions).

- Execution Timing: Background periodic analysis and event-driven on
  multi-party interactions.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. SOCIAL CONTEXT MANAGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Assembles and provides relevant social context to other engines on demand.

- Inputs:
  * Context requests from other engines (Thought, Emotion, Motivation,
    Cognitive Integration).
  * All component outputs (profiles, relationships, interpretations, norms, trust).
  * Memory Engine: Historical context.

- Outputs:
  * SocialContextPackage assembled for the requesting engine.
  * SocialCognitionProvider interface responses.

- Internal State:
  * Context cache: LRU cache of recently assembled packages.

- Algorithms:
  * Context assembly: Collect relevant data from all components, filter by
    requesting engine's needs, freeze into immutable snapshot.
  * Relevance filtering: Include only information above minimum confidence
    thresholds per evidence type.
  * Anti-speculation enforcement: HYPOTHESIZED information excluded or
    explicitly marked with uncertainty flags.

- Execution Timing: Synchronous, on-demand from requesting engines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. SOCIAL LEARNING INTERFACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Coordinates learning evidence dispatch to Learning Engine and applies
owner-directed parameter changes from Learning Engine.

- Inputs:
  * Pattern discovery signals from Social Pattern Recognition Module.
  * Trust evidence from Trust Assessment Module.
  * Norm learning signals from Social Norms Manager.
  * ParameterChangeRequest from Learning Engine (for `social.*` parameters).

- Outputs:
  * SocialAttunementSignal dispatched to Learning Engine.
  * OwnerAcknowledgement and OwnerApplicationReceipt for parameter changes.

- Internal State:
  * Parameter registry: HashMap<parameter_id, ParameterState>.
  * Pending change request queue.

- Algorithms:
  * Signal dispatch: Aggregate evidence from components, format as
    SocialAttunementSignal, dispatch to Learning Engine.
  * Parameter change application: Validate ParameterChangeRequest against
    version guard and valid_range, apply change, increment version, return receipt.
  * Rollback handling: Restore known_good_value, increment version, return
    RollbackReceipt.

- Execution Timing: Asynchronous. Signal dispatch on pattern/evidence accumulation.
  Parameter change application synchronous on receipt.

- Learning Engine v2.5.0 Contract Compliance:
  * Owner engine: SOCIAL.
  * Parameter namespace: `social.*`.
  * Owned parameters include:
    - `social.conversational_verbosity`
    - `social.empathy_weight`
    - `social.trust_initial_baseline`
    - `social.familiarity_growth_rate` ($\alpha_F$)
    - `social.trust_growth_rate` ($\alpha_T^{+}$)
    - `social.trust_decay_rate` ($\alpha_T^{-}$)
    - `social.pattern_detection_threshold`
    - `social.norm_learning_rate`
  * Version-guarded optimistic concurrency: Every parameter maintains a monotonically
    increasing version integer. ParameterChangeRequest must include
    `expected_current_version`; rejected if version mismatch.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. SOCIAL LIFECYCLE MANAGER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Manages social model lifecycle transitions (ACTIVE, COOLING, DORMANT,
ARCHIVED), staleness checks, and garbage collection.

- Inputs:
  * Interaction timestamps from all components.
  * Social relevance scores.
  * Internal State Engine: Resource availability signals.

- Outputs:
  * Lifecycle transition events.
  * Garbage collection actions.
  * Model reconstruction requests to Memory Engine.
  * Staleness notifications to other components.

- Internal State:
  * Model lifecycle registry: HashMap<individual_id, ModelLifecycleState>.
  * Last interaction timestamps.
  * Garbage collection schedule.

- Algorithms:
  * Lifecycle state machine transitions (Section 14.1 of Part 1).
  * Social relevance computation (Section 14.2 of Part 1).
  * Garbage collection sweep: Periodic scan of DORMANT models for ARCHIVED
    transition eligibility.
  * Reconstruction protocol: Retrieve from Memory, rebuild working model,
    apply confidence reduction.

- Decision Rules:
  * NEVER archive models with $R_{social} \ge 0.30$ regardless of inactivity.
  * ALWAYS preserve individual_id and identifiers even after archival.
  * Reconstruction confidence penalty: $C_{reconstructed} = C_{original} \cdot 0.70$.

- Error Handling:
  * Memory Engine unavailable during reconstruction: Create minimal stub profile,
    mark for deferred reconstruction.
  * Concurrent lifecycle transition: Version-guarded state changes.

- Execution Timing: Background periodic sweep (configurable interval, default
  every 100 interactions or once per 24-hour simulated cycle).

PART 3 — INTER-ENGINE COMMUNICATION CONTRACTS
================================================================================

The Social Cognition Engine communicates with ten other engines through formally
defined asynchronous contracts. Each contract specifies the direction, payload
structure, semantic invariants, and boundary rules.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Perception Engine (`PerceptionContextProvider`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Ingress (Perception -> Social Cognition).
Payload:
  * Interaction content (processed text, metadata).
  * Account identity metadata (platform, user IDs).
  * Person-reference extraction (names mentioned, entities referenced).
  * Interaction timestamps and channel identifiers.
  * Contextual signals (tone indicators, formality level).

Semantic Invariants:
  * Perception provides raw observations. Social Cognition interprets them.
  * Perception does NOT resolve identity — it provides signals for identity resolution.
  * Perception does NOT assess social significance — it provides content for assessment.

Boundary Rule: Social Cognition NEVER instructs Perception to filter or modify
incoming observations. Perception is a neutral observation channel.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Memory Engine (`MemoryContextProvider` + `MemoryNominationContract`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Bidirectional.

Ingress (Memory -> Social Cognition):
  * Historical interaction records for specific individuals.
  * Individual-associated memories and experiences.
  * Canonical memory IDs for provenance linkage.
  * Retrieval confidence and relevance scores.
  * Historical relationship context.

Egress (Social Cognition -> Memory):
  * `MemoryNomination`: Significant social experiences nominated for retention.
    - Nomination criteria: interaction_significance $\ge 0.60$ OR relationship
      transition events OR trust-relevant evidence.
    - Nomination payload includes: individual_id, interaction_id, significance_score,
      social_context_snapshot, nominated_at timestamp.
  * Evidence provenance references linking social model attributes to memory records.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Memory owns permanent storage. Social Cognition owns structured interpretation.
  * Social Cognition does NOT duplicate Memory's storage — it references via canonical IDs.
  * When Memory returns "not found," Social Cognition proceeds with available
    working model data only.

Boundary Rule: Social Cognition NEVER deletes or modifies Memory records.
Memory NEVER modifies Social Cognition's working models.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Thought Engine (`ThoughtContextProvider`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Bidirectional.

Ingress (Thought -> Social Cognition):
  * Social hypotheses generated by thought threads.
  * Perspective-related reasoning requests.
  * Reflections on social situations requiring context.

Egress (Social Cognition -> Thought):
  * SocialContextPackage for thought enrichment.
  * Perspective divergence alerts for active social reasoning.
  * Individual profile summaries for thought threads involving social entities.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Thought Engine can generate thoughts about social situations, but Social
    Cognition owns the structured social models those thoughts reference.
  * Social hypotheses generated by Thought Engine are treated as INFERRED evidence
    when incorporated into social models.

Boundary Rule: Thought Engine NEVER directly modifies social models. It dispatches
hypotheses; Social Cognition evaluates and potentially incorporates them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Emotion Engine (`EmotionalContextProvider`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Bidirectional.

Ingress (Emotion -> Social Cognition):
  * Diva's current emotional state relevant to social interactions.
  * Emotional significance signals for social experiences.
  * Empathy-triggered emotional responses (Diva's own empathetic feelings).

Egress (Social Cognition -> Emotion):
  * Social trigger signals: Interaction assessments with emotional_relevance score.
  * Cognitive empathy assessments: Estimated emotional perspectives of others.
  * Social boundary violations (may trigger discomfort or concern).
  * Relationship-relevant emotional triggers (trust breach, closeness development).

Contract: `SocialEmotionalTrigger`:
  - `trigger_id`: UUID.
  - `trigger_type`: Enum (`EMPATHY_SIGNAL`, `TRUST_EVENT`, `RELATIONSHIP_SHIFT`,
    `SOCIAL_BOUNDARY`, `CONFLICT_SIGNAL`, `POSITIVE_SOCIAL_EXPERIENCE`).
  - `interlocutor_id`: UUID.
  - `emotional_relevance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
  - `assessment_context`: Frozen SocialContextPackage subset.
  - `dispatched_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Social Cognition produces cognitive empathy assessments (understanding what
    someone might feel). Emotion Engine produces Diva's emotional response to
    that understanding.
  * Social Cognition NEVER directly sets Diva's emotional state.

Boundary Rule: Emotion Engine owns Diva's emotional states. Social Cognition
provides social stimuli for emotional processing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Motivation Engine (`MotivationContextProvider`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Bidirectional.

Ingress (Motivation -> Social Cognition):
  * Active social drives (social connection drive $D_{social}$, affiliation).
  * Motivation-related social significance (relationship-maintaining motivations).
  * Goal-related social context needs.

Egress (Social Cognition -> Motivation):
  * Social context enriching motivation evaluation.
  * Relationship-based motivation signals (e.g., cooperation opportunity
    with trusted collaborator may enhance motivation).
  * Social commitment obligations (promises made, collaborative agreements).

Contract: `SocialMotivationContext`:
  - `context_id`: UUID.
  - `relevant_relationships`: Tuple of RelationshipRecord summaries.
  - `social_commitments`: Tuple of active commitment records.
  - `cooperation_opportunities`: Tuple of cooperation signals.
  - `dispatched_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Social Cognition provides social context. Motivation Engine independently
    generates and manages social drives.
  * Social Cognition NEVER creates or modifies motivational states.

Boundary Rule: Motivation Engine owns social drives. Social Cognition provides
the social intelligence that informs those drives.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Personality Engine (`PersonalityContextProvider`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Ingress (Personality -> Social Cognition).
Payload:
  * Diva's social tendencies (introversion/extroversion, social warmth,
    attachment style, assertiveness).
  * Constitutional baseline social parameters.
  * Social relevance weight signals (how much Diva's personality emphasizes
    certain relationship types).

Semantic Invariants:
  * Personality provides static or slowly-evolving tendencies. Social Cognition
    uses them to weight social relevance, model prioritization, and empathy
    calibration.
  * Social Cognition can maintain a model of someone else's apparent personality
    traits, but CANNOT change Diva's own Personality Engine parameters.

Boundary Rule: Personality Engine owns Diva's personality traits. Social Cognition
reads them as context, never writes them.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. Learning Engine (`LearningSignalContract`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Bidirectional.

Egress (Social Cognition -> Learning):
  * `SocialAttunementSignal` dispatched per Learning Engine v2.5.0 contract.
  * Signal types: COMMUNICATIVE_MISALIGNMENT, RAPPORT_SHIFT, PATTERN_DISCOVERY,
    TRUST_EVIDENCE, RELATIONSHIP_TRANSITION, NORM_VIOLATION_DETECTED.

Ingress (Learning -> Social Cognition):
  * `ParameterChangeRequest` for `social.*` parameters.
  * Social Cognition validates, applies, and returns `OwnerApplicationReceipt`.
  * `RollbackRequest` for reverting unsuccessful parameter changes.

Contract Details (from Learning Engine v2.5.0 specification):
  * Owner engine: SOCIAL.
  * Parameter namespace: `social.*`.
  * Version-guarded optimistic concurrency on all parameter mutations.
  * Maximum change per update epoch: $\Delta \le 0.05$ for most social parameters.
  * Evidence thresholds per Learning Engine assessment criteria:
    $N \ge 8$ observations minimum for social parameter changes.
    Confidence threshold $\ge 0.80$.
    CUSUM change-point statistic $S_t \ge 4.5$.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Social Cognition dispatches evidence signals. Learning Engine independently
    evaluates whether evidence warrants parameter change.
  * Learning Engine proposes changes. Social Cognition, as owner, applies them.

Boundary Rule: Learning Engine NEVER directly mutates social parameters. Social
Cognition NEVER modifies Learning Engine's assessment algorithms.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. Internal State Engine (`InternalStateProvider`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Ingress (Internal State -> Social Cognition).
Payload:
  * Current cognitive energy level $E_{cog}$.
  * Current cognitive workload $W_{cog}$.
  * Available attention resources.
  * Fatigue level.

Usage in Social Cognition:
  * Low cognitive energy -> Throttle LLM interpretation requests.
  * High cognitive workload -> Defer non-urgent pattern analysis.
  * Very low energy -> Prioritize identity resolution only, defer all
    background social model maintenance.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Internal State Engine owns resource metrics. Social Cognition reads them
    for self-throttling decisions.

Boundary Rule: Social Cognition NEVER modifies Internal State parameters.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. Attention Control (`AttentionContract`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Egress (Social Cognition -> Attention Control).
Payload:
  * Social urgency signals: High-significance social events requiring immediate
    attention allocation (e.g., trust breach detection, relationship crisis signal).
  * Social salience scores for attention prioritization.

Contract: `SocialAttentionSignal`:
  - `signal_id`: UUID.
  - `urgency`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
  - `social_significance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
  - `source_interaction_id`: UUID.
  - `dispatched_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

Semantic Invariants:
  * Social Cognition provides salience signals. Attention Control independently
    arbitrates attention allocation.

Boundary Rule: Social Cognition NEVER directly allocates attention resources.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. Cognitive Integration & Decision Engine (`CognitiveIntegrationContract`)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Direction: Bidirectional.

Egress (Social Cognition -> Cognitive Integration):
  * Comprehensive SocialContextPackage for decision-making.
  * Relationship-informed interaction recommendations.
  * Social norm applicability assessments.
  * Trust-based reliability assessments for decision inputs.
  * Perspective divergence alerts.
  * Social uncertainty and confidence information.

Ingress (Cognitive Integration -> Social Cognition):
  * Decision outcomes affecting social models (e.g., Diva decided to maintain
    the relationship -> adjust relationship status accordingly).
  * Interaction outcome feedback (success/failure of socially-informed decisions).

Semantic Invariants:
  * Social Cognition provides the social intelligence. Cognitive Integration
    makes the decisions.
  * Social Cognition NEVER independently decides Diva's actions or communication.

Boundary Rule: Social Cognition cannot unilaterally decide whether Diva should
forgive, confront, ignore, or continue making plans with someone. Those are
Cognitive Integration decisions.

PART 4 — COMPREHENSIVE DATA DICTIONARY & RELATIONAL PERSISTENCE
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION A: SCHEMAS FOR ALL 23 INTERNAL DATA STRUCTURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All data models are deeply immutable structures (frozen dataclasses / Pydantic
models with `frozen=True` and recursive immutability of nested collections via
tuples, frozensets, and MappingProxyType).

1. `IndividualProfile`:
   - `individual_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `identity_type`: Enum (`PERSISTENT`, `TEMPORARY`, `INDIRECT`, `COLLECTIVE`).
   - `entity_type`: Enum (`PERSON`, `ORGANIZATION`, `GROUP`, `FICTIONAL`).
   - `display_names`: Immutable Tuple of String.
   - `identity_confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `lifecycle_state`: Enum (`ACTIVE`, `COOLING`, `DORMANT`, `ARCHIVED`).
   - `social_relevance`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `first_interaction_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.
   - `last_interaction_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.
   - `total_interaction_count`: Integer.
   - `profile_version`: Integer (monotonically increasing).
   - `created_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.
   - `updated_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

2. `IdentityRecord`:
   - `record_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `individual_id`: UUID (FK to IndividualProfile).
   - `identifier_type`: Enum (`ACCOUNT_ID`, `PLATFORM_HANDLE`, `EMAIL`,
     `DISPLAY_NAME`, `CUSTOM`).
   - `identifier_value`: String.
   - `platform`: Optional String.
   - `confidence`: Float in [0.0, 1.0].
   - `source_type`: Enum (`EXPLICIT`, `OBSERVED`, `INFERRED`).
   - `created_at`: ISO-8601 UTC.

3. `IdentityEvidence`:
   - `evidence_id`: UUID (Primary Key).
   - `individual_id`: UUID (FK to IndividualProfile).

     belief_content: "person_F33 believes project_X is still active."
     belief_confidence: 0.80 (high — based on their last known information).
     truth_assessment: LIKELY_FALSE (Diva knows it's cancelled).
   - Social Context Manager: Assembles SocialContextPackage including the

   - Emotion Engine: Receives SocialEmotionalTrigger noting the delicate social

4. Expected State Transitions:
   - PerspectiveRecord created (type=EPISTEMIC, diva_perspective_differs=True).
   - BeliefEstimate created (LIKELY_FALSE truth assessment).

   - INSERT into individual_belief_models.
   - INSERT into interaction_records.
   - INSERT into social_evidence_records (perspective divergence evidence).
   - INSERT into social_model_history (knowledge model updated).

   - Social Cognition IDENTIFIES the perspective divergence. It does NOT decide

   - Emotion Engine independently evaluates Diva's emotional reaction to the situation.

   - Cognitive Integration receives full context including the perspective gap.
   - person_F33's belief model explicitly marked as LIKELY_FALSE.

   - If person_F33 reveals they already know about the cancellation: Update

SCENARIO 12: CONTRADICTORY EVIDENCE (CONFIDENCE REVISION & UNCERTAINTY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Initial State:
   - person_K88 has established attribute: "enjoys outdoor activities"
     (INFERRED, $C=0.72$, evidence_count=5 from discussion patterns).
   - person_K88 explicitly states: "I actually don't enjoy being outdoors. I
     prefer staying inside."

2. Trigger:
   - Direct contradictory statement by person_K88 during interaction.

3. Expected Subsystem Interactions:
   - Individual Modeling Module: Detects contradiction with existing attribute.
     1. Records contradictory evidence (source_type=SELF_REPORT).
     2. Existing attribute confidence reduced: $C_{new} = 0.72 - 0.15 = 0.57$.
     3. ContradictionRecord created.
     4. Second contradictory instance check: This is the first direct contradiction.
        Attribute NOT yet marked DISPUTED (requires $C < 0.20$ or $\ge 3$
        contradictory instances).
   - New attribute created: "prefers indoor activities" (REPORTED by subject,
     $C=0.80$, source_type=SELF_REPORT).
   - Re-evaluation: Checks whether the outdoor inference was based on discussion
     patterns that might mean something else (discussing outdoor topics ≠ enjoying
     outdoors).
   - Social Pattern Recognition: Updates any patterns that referenced the
     "outdoor enthusiast" attribute.

4. Expected State Transitions:
   - IndividualAttribute ("outdoor activities"): confidence 0.72 -> 0.57,
     contradiction_flag = True.
   - IndividualAttribute ("prefers indoor"): Created (ACTIVE, $C=0.80$).
   - SocialModelHistory entry created.

5. Expected Persistence Changes:
   - UPDATE individual_attributes ("outdoor" -> $C=0.57$, contradiction_flag=1).
   - INSERT into individual_attributes ("indoor preference", $C=0.80$).
   - INSERT into social_evidence_records (SELF_REPORT contradicting inference).
   - INSERT into social_model_history (CORRECTED).

6. Ownership-Boundary Checks:
   - Self-report takes priority over behavioral inference.
   - Existing attribute NOT deleted — confidence reduced and flagged.
   - Both pieces of information coexist until further evidence resolves.

7. Expected Result:
   - Both attributes coexist: "outdoor" at reduced confidence ($C=0.57$,
     contradicted) and "indoor" at higher confidence ($C=0.80$, self-reported).
   - Future SocialContextPackages will present the indoor preference as the
     higher-confidence attribute.
   - If person_K88 later discusses hiking enthusiastically, the outdoor attribute's
     confidence may recover — the system allows revision in both directions.

8. Failure Scenario & Recovery Behavior:
   - If further contradictions accumulate ($\ge 3$): "outdoor" attribute marked
     DISPUTED, excluded from active context packages.
   - If person_K88's behavior later confirms outdoor interest (direct observation):
     Both attributes maintained; "outdoor" may be re-evaluated with new evidence.
     The person may genuinely have mixed feelings (doesn't enjoy general outdoors
     but enjoys specific outdoor activities).
