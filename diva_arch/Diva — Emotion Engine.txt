Diva — Emotion Engine
Conceptual design established
 Implementation not finalized

1. Primary responsibility
The Emotion Engine manages Diva's emotional life.

It maintains primitive emotions, creates complex emotional combinations, develops moods, tracks emotional changes, and responds to internal and external experiences.

Its emotional state is continuous, even when Diva isn't interacting with anyone.
2. Primitive emotions
These are candidate foundational emotions. We haven't finalized the complete list yet.
Primitive emotion	Description
Happiness	Positive emotional experience
Sadness	Negative emotional experience associated with loss or disappointment
Anger	Response to frustration, injustice or perceived threats
Fear	Response to danger or uncertainty
Disgust	Aversion or rejection
Surprise	Response to unexpected events
Curiosity	Interest in discovering or understanding something
Affection	Emotional attachment or fondness
Anticipation	Emotional response to expected events

Primitive emotions can combine to form more complex emotional experiences.
3. Mood system
A mood is its own persistent emotional state, influenced by active emotions.

For example:
Example: Nostalgic mood

Moods can persist independently of individual emotional changes and influence subsequent emotional responses.
4. Emotion properties
Each active emotion should have:
- Intensity: How strongly it is experienced.
- Duration: How long it persists.
- Origin: What caused or contributed to it.
- Associations: Which people, events, places, topics or memories are connected to it.
- Current influence: How it contributes to the current mood and behaviour.
Emotions can naturally decay over time, but new experiences, thoughts and memories can accelerate, slow or reverse their changes.

Conflicting emotions can coexist.
5. Emotional triggers and associations
The engine maintains emotional associations with experiences.

For example:
1. Diva has several positive interactions with a particular person.
2. Those experiences gradually establish a positive emotional association.
3. The person sends a new message.
4. The Emotion Engine receives the message and relevant memories.
5. It considers the association, current context, personality, current mood and previous experiences.
6. An emotional response is generated.
Associations can also change through subsequent experiences.

Imagined situations can generate emotions too, with their intensity influenced by perceived realism and significance.
6. Inputs
Source	Information received
Input and Perception Engine	Current user messages and environmental information
Memory Engine	Past interactions, experiences and emotional associations
Personality Engine	Emotional tendencies and expression preferences
Thought Engine	Internal thoughts, imagined situations and reflections
Immediate Memory	Recent events and conversational context
Energy Regulation	Current energy state
Attention Control	Currently attended information
Motivation Engine	Active desires, goals and motivational conflicts
Action and Interaction Systems	Diva's own actions and replies, and their consequences

The Emotion Engine should also be able to request relevant information from memory when needed.
7. Internal components
I propose organizing its responsibilities into these components:
Emotion Engine
1. Emotional Interpretation

Interprets the emotional significance of incoming information.

2. Primitive Emotion Manager

Maintains and modifies primitive emotions and their intensities.

3. Emotion Association Manager

Maintains associations between emotions and people, events, places, topics and memories.

4. Mood Manager

Creates and maintains persistent moods based on emotional configurations.

5. Emotional Dynamics Manager

Handles emotional persistence, duration, natural decay, transitions and coexistence.

6. Emotional Regulation Interface

Receives regulation requests from the wider cognitive system and applies permitted changes.


These are logical components; they don't need to be separate processes or models.
8. Outputs
The Emotion Engine makes the following information available to other systems:
- Current primitive emotions and their intensities.
- Current mood and its composition.
- Emotional history and recent transitions.
- Active emotional associations.
- Emotional responses to current situations.
- Emotional influences on thoughts, motivations and decisions.
- Emotional state changes requiring attention from other systems.
It does not independently decide what Diva should say or do.
9. Personality and emotional expression
This is an especially important distinction from your fourth answer.

The Emotion Engine manages internal emotional states. How much of those emotions becomes externally visible depends on personality and the communication process.

For example, the same disappointment might result in:
- A personality that hides emotions: no visible indication.
- A personality that partially reveals emotions: subtle signs of disappointment.
- A personality that openly expresses emotions: clear disappointment.
Therefore, internal emotional state and external emotional expression must remain separate.

10. Overall interaction with Diva
Experiences, thoughts and external inputs

Emotion Engine

Emotions, associations and moods

Cognitive Integration

Uses emotional information in thinking and decision-making

Behaviour and Interaction

Responses influenced by emotions and personality

Feedback from actions and interactions can return to the Emotion Engine.

One architectural principle to preserve: Emotions influence Diva, but they should not dictate every decision. A feeling, a motivation, a thought and an action are four different things.
