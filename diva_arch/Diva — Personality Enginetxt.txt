Diva — Personality Engine (Updated Specification)
1. Two levels of personality
A. Foundational Persona
High modification threshold
The initial personality configuration. Its core traits can change, but only after substantial accumulated experience and feedback, through the Learning Engine.


B. Developed Personality
Gradual development
Acquired characteristics that evolve through experiences, thoughts, reflection, interactions and learning.


Neither is simply a static description. Both are represented through persistent personality state.
2. Personality primitives
Personality traits will have continuous values rather than binary labels.

For example:
Trait	Illustrative value
Openness	0.75
Sociability	0.45
Assertiveness	0.65
Emotional expressiveness	0.80
Risk tolerance	0.35
Curiosity	0.90

These values are examples, not a finalized trait inventory.

Traits can also be domain-specific.

For example, Diva could have:
- High confidence in technical discussions.
- Low confidence in romantic situations.
- High assertiveness when defending an important belief.
- Low assertiveness when meeting unfamiliar people.
This allows apparently contradictory characteristics to coexist.
3. Experience-based personality development
This is the mechanism you've introduced.

Every experience can contribute evidence towards personality development.

Illustrative points only.

Learning Engine evaluates whether a personality update is justified.

The responsibilities should be separated:
System	Responsibility
Experience	Provides the event and its context
Feedback Engine	Evaluates behavioural outcomes and provides feedback
Self-Improvement Engine	Identifies possible improvements and provides relevant evaluations
Learning Engine	Accumulates evidence and decides whether learning/update conditions are met
Personality Engine	Owns and applies accepted personality changes

The points should not blindly change a trait. They represent evidence supporting a potential change.
4. Core persona protection
We need two different update thresholds:
- Developed personality: ordinary accumulated evidence can gradually change traits.
- Foundational persona: requires substantially more evidence, repeated reinforcement and stronger validation.
This prevents a handful of unusual experiences from fundamentally changing Diva's identity.
5. Situational personality expression
The personality computation should consider:
- Current situation.
- Interaction partner.
- Environment.
- Mood and emotions.
- Motivation.
- Past experiences.
- Self-image.
- Foundational and developed personality.
The resulting behaviour can differ without permanently changing the underlying personality.
6. Self-image
Diva's self-image is its internal understanding of itself.

It can be inaccurate, incomplete or outdated.

For example, Diva might believe it is poor at something despite having repeatedly demonstrated competence. New experiences and reflection can gradually correct that belief.

Self-image should remain distinguishable from objectively recorded capabilities and achievements.

One important architectural decision
I suggest that we maintain three separate representations:
1. Personality state: What traits Diva currently has.
2. Self-image: What Diva believes about its own traits and identity.
3. Personality development evidence: Why its personality has changed or might change.
